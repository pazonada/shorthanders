## Target customer

**Primary: law firms and attorneys** (the demand side) – explicitly calls out contingency-fee/plaintiff firms managing cash flow. **Secondary: court reporters and videographers**, engaged as independent contractors who pick up jobs Steno has already booked, not as a self-serve open marketplace. Unlike DS, Steno's core business is selling reporting *services* to firms – reporters are Steno's supply chain, not independent businesses Steno merely helps get found.

---

## Pricing model

- **Model type (firms):** quote/contact-based. No public per-page or appearance-fee rate card found on the homepage – standard for the agency model, and a direct contrast to DS's transparency positioning.
- **DelayPay (financing product):** deferred-payment option for firms. Site language: enables clients to "focus on winning cases without worrying about cash flow" with "transparent, interest-free pricing," payment "deferred until case settlement," and explicitly "not dependent on the size of the recovery." This is a real point of differentiation from DS – it's a financing product, not a marketplace feature, and it only makes sense for an agency that controls the invoice relationship end to end.
- **Model type (reporters):** independent-contractor engagement, not a listing fee. Reporters browse and select jobs from a "Provider Dashboard and Job Marketplace" rather than being emailed assignments – but the jobs originate from Steno's own client relationships, not the reporter's own book of business.
- **Reporter payment terms:** site claims "prompt payment in 10 days or less after receiving your transcript, and always paying upfront for copy orders." Not independently verified beyond the site's own claim and one on-site testimonial.

## Service scope

Full-service litigation-support agency, not a single-purpose tool:
- Court reporting (depositions; site copy emphasizes litigation support broadly, with California-specific language in places)
- Remote depositions via "Steno Connect" (Zoom integration, exhibit handling)
- Videography
- Certified interpreters
- Equipment rental and conference-room booking
- Same-day transcript delivery (rush service)
- AI-generated transcription product ("Transcript Genius," released 2024, positioned as a generative-AI transcription/document-management tool layered alongside human reporters, not a replacement)
- DelayPay financing (above)
- 24/7 "Firm Dashboard" for scheduling, file access, and invoice management

This is meaningfully broader than DS's scope (directory + messaging + reporter get-paid tools) or than either other direct-competitor entry (CSR Suite, On The Record – both reporter-side practice-management tools only). Steno is not comparable feature-for-feature; it's a different kind of business (full agency) wearing modern software.

Source: [steno.com homepage](https://steno.com/)

## CSR-supply model

Steno **books the reporter on the firm's behalf** – it is the intermediary in the transaction, the same structural role a traditional agency plays. Reporters are independent contractors who select jobs from a Steno-run job marketplace, but the client relationship (the law firm) belongs to Steno, not to the reporter. This is the single most important structural fact for DS's positioning: Steno is a tech-modern version of the exact agency model DS is built to disintermediate. A reporter using Steno does not keep or grow their own direct client relationships through the platform the way a reporter using DS does.

Source: [steno.com/court-reporters](https://steno.com/court-reporters)

## Strengths vs. DS

- **Real money and real scale.** Roughly $80M+ in disclosed funding across rounds (see caveat above on not summing precisely), an active national expansion, and years of operating history. DS has none of this and shouldn't pretend otherwise.
- **Full-service convenience.** One dashboard covers reporters, videographers, interpreters, equipment, and AI transcription – a firm that wants a single vendor for everything gets that from Steno and would need to piece it together from DS (directory/messaging/payments only) plus other tools.
- **DelayPay is a genuine differentiator DS cannot replicate.** Deferred, interest-free payment tied to case settlement is only possible because Steno controls the full invoice relationship as the agency of record – it requires exactly the intermediary role DS deliberately doesn't play.
- **Brand polish and marketing budget.** Professional press presence, an active content site ("The Brief"), G2 reviews, and paid growth channels DS can't currently match.
- **AI transcription product (Transcript Genius)** – a real technology investment DS has no equivalent to and isn't pursuing.

---

## Weaknesses vs. DS

- **Structurally an agency, however modern the software.** Steno takes the booking, sets the terms, and stands between the firm and the reporter. It cannot credibly offer "keep your own clients, keep more of your rate" – that would cannibalize its own revenue model. DS's core positioning line lands harder specifically because of this contrast: *"We're not an agency – you keep your clients, your rates, and your relationships; we just make you findable and get you paid."*
- **No visible public pricing for firms.** Quote-based pricing is normal for an agency but is exactly the opacity DS positions against.
- **Reporters don't own the relationship.** A reporter on Steno's marketplace is picking up Steno's jobs on Steno's terms, not building their own independent client base the way a reporter using DS's directory + direct messaging does.
- **Broad, expensive-to-replicate scope may also mean less focus.** Steno is simultaneously a staffing agency, an AI transcription company, and a fintech lender (DelayPay). DS's narrower, neutral-facilitator scope is a genuine focus advantage, not just a resource constraint.

## Overlap with our positioning

| DS messaging pillar | Steno overlap? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Transparent pricing / no hidden fees | No | Quote-based, agency-typical opacity |
| No agency cut | **No – this is the core contrast.** | Steno *is* the agency; DS is built specifically not to be one |
| Direct CSR-to-attorney connection, reporter keeps the relationship | No | Reporters work Steno's job marketplace, not their own book of business |
| Verified credentials | Partial | RPR/credential display not confirmed on Steno's public pages this pass; not a stated pillar of their marketing |
| California-specific compliance | No | Nationwide, not CA-specific |
| Simple / fast / modern UX | Yes | Real overlap – Steno's dashboard, fast-pay claims, and app-like feel are genuinely modern |
| Neutral facilitator, not a service provider | No | Directly opposite model – Steno is the service provider |

**Strategic read:** Steno is not a head-to-head threat to DS's actual model – it's the best available proof that a funded, credible player believes there's real demand for a better court-reporting experience than the old-line agencies. That's market validation, not competitive collision, because Steno's whole business depends on being the intermediary and DS's whole business depends on not being one. The realistic long-term watch-item is that a funded agency like Steno could bolt a directory/self-serve marketplace layer onto its existing platform – but doing so credibly would require Steno to let reporters route around Steno's own booking and fee structure, which cuts against its business model. DS's neutrality is authentic in a way an agency-turned-marketplace can't easily fake.
