### Phrases worth flagging

**"Finally simple" family of phrasing** is not used by any current competitor. However, "simple" and "simplified" are extremely common in legal tech hero copy broadly (not just this competitive set). The word is not distinctive over time – it describes a desired state, not a differentiator.

**"Workflow" / "manage your workflow"** appears in On The Record and is a common legal-tech cliché. DS doesn't use it, which is good.

**"Transparent" / "transparent pricing"** – DS uses this in the lede but not the tagline. No competitor in this set leads with it in their hero. This is a potential headline differentiator DS currently buries in subtext.

**"Statutory" / "statutory rates"** – Not used in any competitor hero copy. This is genuinely differentiating vocabulary that DS currently uses in the lede ("Transparent statutory pricing") rather than in the headline.

---

## Assessment: does the current tagline need to change?

**The good news:** No competitor is currently using "simple," "finally," or "finally simple" in their hero. On a strict word-collision test, DS's tagline is clean today.

**The honest concern:** "Court transcripts, finally simple" describes a desired experience rather than a specific differentiator. It could apply to any legal-tech product that makes transcripts easier to get. If a competitor with more brand recognition adopted similar language, DS would be the one that sounds derivative. The tagline is generic enough that it does not distinguish DS from the category of "legal tech that simplifies things."

**The deeper issue:** DS's actual differentiators – statutory rates, no agency cut, CA court-specific scope, verified CSR credentials – appear in the lede, not the headline. The headline currently advertises a feeling; the lede advertises the specifics. For a B2B product targeting legal assistants who evaluate platforms on operational criteria, leading with the feeling and burying the differentiator is a positioning miss.

**Recommendation:** The tagline is worth updating – not because of current word collision, but because we can be more specific without being harder to read. See alternatives below.

---

## Proposed alternative taglines

Each alternative is paired with a recommended kicker, because the kicker is load-bearing context. All use en-dashes (–) only.

---

### Option 1: "The direct line to your court reporter."
**Kicker:** "Now onboarding founding firms and reporters"
**Rationale:** "Direct line" does double duty – it signals the marketplace removes the intermediary (no agency) and evokes the practical, unmediated relationship between attorney and reporter. The word "direct" is load-bearing: it describes the product architecture (no agency in the middle), the pricing model (statutory rates, not agency markup), and the UX (book and pay in one place). Audience-honest: written toward the attorney/legal assistant ("your court reporter" frames the reporter as belonging to the firm's workflow). No competitor in the current set uses "direct" in their hero.

---

### Option 2: "CA court transcripts at the statutory rate – nothing more."
**Kicker:** "Now onboarding founding firms and reporters"
**Rationale:** Extremely specific. "Statutory rate" is the core differentiation claim and the only thing DS can promise that competitors literally cannot (because they're not structured around it). "Nothing more" addresses the hidden-fee anxiety that legal assistants feel when dealing with agencies. This option uses price as a supporting signal (statutory = the legal maximum, not a discount), which is consistent with our decision to not lead with a promotional price. Risk: it's long for a headline and reads like a policy statement more than a brand line. Works better as a sub-headline than a top-line H1.

---

### Option 3: "Court reporters you know, transcripts you trust."
**Kicker:** "Now onboarding founding firms and reporters"
**Rationale:** The repetition ("you know ... you trust") creates rhythm and points at the transparency angle without being abstract about it. "Know" = verified credentials, named reporter profiles (attorneys can see who they're getting). "Trust" = statutory pricing, no surprise invoices, delivery you can count on. Audience-honest: addresses the attorney/legal assistant buyer. No competitor uses this construction. Risk: "you trust" is vague enough that it could apply to any service-provider marketplace.

---

### Option 4: "Order a court transcript. Not a favor."
**Kicker:** "Now onboarding founding firms and reporters"
**Rationale:** The wry framing captures the real pain point: the current process of getting a transcript from an agency often feels like navigating personal relationships, opaque availability, and unclear timelines – like calling in a favor rather than using a tool. DS makes it transactional (in the good sense: clear, fast, repeatable). Sharp voice that fits DS's "direct, not bro-y" brand tone. Audience-honest: the "favor" framing will resonate immediately with legal assistants who deal with agencies. No competitor uses this angle. Risk: slightly edgy for a category that tends toward professional/formal register; may need testing.

---

### Option 5: "The court reporter marketplace built for California."
**Kicker:** "Now onboarding founding firms and reporters"
**Rationale:** Maximally specific. "Marketplace" sets the product category accurately (not an agency, not a staffing service). "Built for California" signals CA statutory rate compliance, CRB verification, and public-hearing focus – all things competitors can't claim. For the audience most likely to find DS through search, this is highly indexable. Risk: "built for California" may feel limiting if/when DS expands to other states. "For now" is implicit but not stated. Also: "marketplace" as a word is less evocative than some alternatives.

---

### Option 6 (pair with new kicker): "Verified reporters. Statutory rates. Public-record transcripts."
**Kicker:** "California court transcripts for law firms and reporters"
**Rationale:** Serial construction – three short claims, each naming a distinct differentiator. None of these phrases appear in any competitor's hero. Each claim is verifiable and honest: we verify CSRs via CRB, we price at statutory rates, and we serve public-record proceedings (which distinguishes us from deposition-only competitors). This works well as a bold headline with each phrase on its own line. Risk: reads more like a feature list than a brand line; may feel like a FAQ answer rather than a headline.

---

## Top recommendation

**Option 4: "Order a court transcript. Not a favor."**

Rationale: It's the only option that names the specific emotional pain point (current process is opaque and relationship-dependent) and makes DS's value proposition clear through contrast (transactional = good, not impersonal). It is genuinely distinctive from all current competitor hero copy. It pairs naturally with the existing kicker without modification. It's short, memorable, and calibrated to the legal-assistant buyer who lives this frustration daily.

If the tone is judged too sharp for the professional register, the runner-up is **Option 3** ("Court reporters you know, transcripts you trust") – which covers the same transparency differentiator with softer language and stronger rhythm.

**This is a recommendation for Aud and Rosie to evaluate** – both voice calibration and legal-community reception should inform the final call.
