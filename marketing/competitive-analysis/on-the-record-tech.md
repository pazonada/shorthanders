## Target customer

**Primary: court reporters.** Secondary: scopists and proofreaders who collaborate with reporters. Attorneys and law firms are not addressed on any visible page – there is no demand-side product.

The "MarketPlace" tab in-product is a reporter-to-scopist matching tool (browse profiles, view availability, find support staff) – not an attorney-to-reporter marketplace.

## Service scope

**Reporter-side practice management.** Four primary modules:
1. **Invoicing** – create/send invoices, log payments and expenses
2. **Jobs** – track jobs from start to finish
3. **Collaborations** – file sharing, comments, and chat with support staff (scopists, proofreaders)
4. **MarketPlace** – browse profiles, view availability, find scopists/proofreaders (reporter-to-support-staff, not reporter-to-attorney)

No live-attendance booking, no attorney-facing order workflow, no statutory rate enforcement. Scope is entirely reporter-side business management.

## CSR-supply model

On The Record is not an attorney-facing marketplace. Reporters list themselves in the MarketPlace to be found by other reporters' support staff – not by attorneys. No credential verification visible.

## Strengths vs. DS

- **Live product with clear pricing.** Attorneys who try to Google alternatives to agencies may encounter this before DS if they're looking for "court reporter software."
- **Reporter trust via founder identity.** Valerie is a working CSR who built this from lived experience – same founder-credibility play as CSR Suite.
- **Scopist/proofreader network.** If they have a functioning marketplace of scopists, they have a real supply-side community DS does not yet have.
- **Affordable entry.** $35/month or $300/year is low friction for reporters already running their own business. May compete with our $99/year listing fee in reporters' minds even though the products are different.
- **14-day free trial, no credit card.** Lower friction than DS's upfront listing fee.

---

## Weaknesses vs. DS

- **No demand side.** Attorneys and legal assistants cannot use On The Record. There is no product for the firm. This is a practice-management tool, not a marketplace.
- **No court-hearing focus.** The workflow is built around depositions and transcript production. No evidence of public-hearing attendance, statutory rate compliance, or CA CRB verification.
- **Geographic ambiguity.** No CA-specific compliance posture. DS's CA-specific positioning (statutory rates, CRB verification) is a meaningful differentiator for our launch window.
- **MarketPlace is reporter-to-support-staff.** Their "MarketPlace" label could create confusion if attorneys search for it, but the product doesn't serve them. If DS gets compared to On The Record, we win on the "brings you new work" dimension immediately.
- **Subscription model vs. transaction-fee model.** For a reporter who picks up occasional DS jobs, DS costs nothing upfront ($99/yr) and only charges when paid. If a reporter is just starting out, our model may be lower risk.
