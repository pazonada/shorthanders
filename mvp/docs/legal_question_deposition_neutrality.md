# Legal question for counsel: deposition-reporter neutrality

**Purpose:** a plain-English brief to share with a lawyer, and to keep on file. Rosie
(family-law advisor) can read it now for context; the actual opinion needs a lawyer who
knows court-reporter regulation.

## What Dewey Sholes is (context for the lawyer)

A website that (1) lists California court reporters in a searchable directory, (2) lets law
firms message them, and (3) lets reporters send invoices and collect payment through the
site. We are **not** a court reporting agency – we don't employ reporters, assign work, or
take part in the actual job. The firm and the reporter agree terms directly between
themselves. It's a one-person business (Studio Crux, LLC).

How we plan to make money:
- **Firms** pay a monthly subscription to see full profiles and message reporters.
- **Reporters** list for free; they pay a small fee only to collect payment through us –
  either a cut of each payment, or an optional flat monthly plan.

## The concern (plain English)

A **deposition** reporter is a neutral sworn officer – they swear in the witness and must
stay impartial between the two sides of a case. California has rules protecting that
neutrality, including rules and a history of disputes about reporting companies giving
attorneys perks, discounts, or having money arrangements with one side that could bias the
neutral reporter.

Our worry: **we take money from both sides** (firms pay us, reporters pay us). Could that be
seen as compromising a deposition reporter's required neutrality, or as a prohibited
incentive or an undisclosed arrangement?

Scope, to be clear:
- This is **only about depositions** (the neutral-officer role) – not court-audio transcription.
- It's about our **money setup**, not about hosting messaging or the deals people make through
  the site (we believe that part is fine – same as people making deals over email or LinkedIn).
- It only matters **if and when reporters collect deposition payments through us.**

## The question(s) to ask

> Does taking money from both sides – law firms paying a subscription, and reporters paying a
> fee to collect payment through us – run afoul of California's court-reporter **neutrality,
> anti-incentive, or financial-disclosure** rules for **depositions**? Could it be seen as
> compromising a deposition reporter's neutrality as a sworn officer, or as a prohibited
> incentive or undisclosed arrangement?
>
> If there's any risk: what disclosures or structural changes would keep us clear? And does it
> matter whether the reporter fee is a **flat subscription** versus a **cut of each payment**?

That last part matters: the answer might be *"a flat plan is fine, but a per-deposition cut is
a problem,"* which would tell us how to structure the reporter fee for deposition work.

## Who to ask

A lawyer who knows **court-reporting / deposition regulation in California** – not a general
or family-law attorney. (Rosie is family-law; she's an advisor, not the right person to
answer this specific question.)
