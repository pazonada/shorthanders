# Getting the first ~50 firms — a plain plan

Built on the old marketing research (target list, practice areas, geography, the paralegal-as-
buyer insight), adjusted for the reduced MVP (a directory + get-paid tools, no matching). The
old firm targeting still holds; the old *pitch and incentives* don't, because they were built
around jobs/orders/fees the product no longer has.

## The one thing to get right first: supply before demand

**You can't sell a firm a subscription to an empty directory.** In the old "we'll match you"
model, you could sell firms before supply was deep. Not anymore — a firm paying to search a
directory needs to see real reporters, with filled-out profiles, in their county. So:

**Step 0 (before chasing firms): get reporters to claim profiles in the launch counties.** It's
free and easy for them (they want to be found + get the get-paid tools), and it's what makes
the firm subscription worth buying. Aim for enough claimed reporters in the Bay Area counties
that the directory looks alive there. This also unlocks the best firm channel (below).

## Who to target (from the research — still valid)

- **Small-to-midsize California litigation firms, ~2–20 attorneys** (sweet spot 10–30), where a
  **paralegal or office manager picks the vendors** without committee sign-off.
- **Practice areas that actually use court reporters,** roughly in order of how much:
  insurance defense, PI plaintiff, family law (Rosie's strongest network), employment, medical
  malpractice, general civil/commercial litigation, probate/trust litigation.
- **Skip:** BigLaw (100+ attorneys), transactional-only firms (M&A, real estate, securities),
  immigration, pure criminal defense, workers'-comp-only, ADR-only boutiques.
- **Geography: Bay Area first — Santa Clara, Contra Costa, Alameda, and SF.** SF is high priority
  because Rosie can attend SF paralegal events in person. LA waits (entrenched depo vendors,
  operationally heavy).

## Who to actually sell to

**The paralegal / legal assistant / office manager — not the partner.** They're the one who
finds and books reporters today. The whole game is being the easiest option for that person.

## Channels, best-first (all near-zero budget)

1. **Reporter-to-firm intros — warmest, and now the most on-model.** Every reporter who claims a
   profile already works with a handful of firms. Ask them to tell those firms "find me on Dewey
   Sholes." A reporter vouching to their own attorney clients is the cheapest, strongest channel,
   and it seeds both sides at once. Give them something easy to hand over (their profile link;
   later, the printed business card idea from the roadmap).
2. **Rosie's warm network** — her family-law and paralegal contacts; she personally introduces DS.
3. **SF / Bay Area paralegal events and associations** — in person, aimed right at the buyer.
4. **Cold email to paralegals** at the pre-researched firms (see assets) once contacts are enriched.
5. **LinkedIn + local bar-association listservs** — low-cost presence/content.

## The offer — how to get a "yes" for a paid subscription

- **Free trial** (say 14–30 days). Essential — let them feel the value before paying.
- **Founding-firm deal** — early firms get a locked-in low rate (or a few months free), direct
  founder access, and a say in the roadmap. (The old incentive programs were tied to "orders"
  and a platform fee that no longer exist — re-base any incentive to subscription sign-up.)
- **Lead with the concrete pain:** "Stop calling agencies and paying their markup. Here's every
  reporter in your county — message them directly, pay them cleanly." Targets firms already
  inclined to hire direct.

## The sequence
1. Seed reporter supply in the Bay Area counties (free claims).
2. Turn each claimed reporter into a referral — they tell the firms they work with.
3. Enrich the 26-firm seed list (paralegal names/emails) and expand it in the launch counties.
4. Warm outreach first (Rosie + reporter intros), cold email second.
5. Offer the free trial + founding-firm deal.
6. Track trial → paying in the tracker (re-based to subscription events, not orders).
7. Concentrate all ~50 firms in the counties where you have reporter density — don't spread thin.

## What we're dropping from the old plan (and why)
- **The insurance-carrier "preferred vendor" scheme** — it relied on auditable per-case
  cost-savings data, volume routing, and a cost dashboard the directory no longer produces. Shelve
  it; maybe revisit much later as a loose "preferred directory" referral, not a savings/volume play.
  (Your instinct to drop it is right.)
- **Order-based incentives** (points per order, "$25 on first job," fee-free first order) — there
  are no in-platform orders or platform fee. Re-base to sign-up / invoices-paid, or retire.
- **The "12% undercuts the agency markup" pitch** — obsolete. New pitch is "skip the agency markup,
  and the subscription is cheap."

## Existing asset to reuse

count, practice areas, city, tier, CSR-relationship signal). Contact fields (paralegal names/

## The honest caveat
The directory is a **harder** firm sell than the old "we match you" model, because the firm does
more of the work themselves. That's why the reporter-intro channel and the low price matter so
much: a warm intro from a reporter the firm already trusts, plus a cheap trial, is what overcomes
"I'll just call my agency."
