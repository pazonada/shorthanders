# Marketing & outreach – Dewey Sholes (reduced-scope MVP)

The tactical folder index. The one-page *direction* is [growth_strategy.md](growth_strategy.md);
read it first. Built for the reality we're in: **two people, ~$0 budget, no staff. Supply runs
statewide; only our hands-on firm-selling concentrates (Bay Area first, then LA).**

## What we're selling, and to whom

A **monthly subscription** that lets a law firm search a directory of California
court reporters, message them directly, and skip the agency middleman. The reporter
side is free to list (pay-per-invoice to get paid, or a flat Pro plan for heavy users).

**The buyer is not the partner.** It's the **paralegal, legal secretary, or office
manager** who actually finds and books reporters. At a small firm that person picks
the vendor without committee sign-off. Every tactic below aims at that person.

## The sequence (how growth actually happens)

1. **Statewide supply, free and automatic.** The directory already lists every licensed CA
   reporter (public data), and the CSR email invites all of them to claim. Claiming is free;
   it's what makes the directory look alive and what powers the next step. Reporter side:
   [`mvp/docs/csr_email_outreach.md`](../docs/csr_email_outreach.md) + the in-app claim flow.
2. **Reporters pull in their own firms – statewide.** Every reporter who claims already works
   with a handful of firms. The in-app "invite a firm" flow lets them bring those firms on
   directly, seeding a market in *their* metro without us selling there. This is the #1 channel
   and our real statewide engine – so the "invite the firms you already work with" ask has to
   be loud and central at claim time.
3. **We sell firms by hand – concentrated.** Postcards, Rosie's network, paralegal events. Two
   people can't do this in 40 metros, so it points where reporter density and our reach overlap:
   Bay Area first, then LA. This is what [firm_outreach_playbook.md](firm_outreach_playbook.md) covers.

## Reaching reporters (the supply side)

Two channels, both free and both statewide:

1. **The one-time claim-invite email** – [`mvp/docs/csr_email_outreach.md`](../docs/csr_email_outreach.md).
2. **Facebook groups** – CA court reporters congregate there professionally; it's where our own
   WC rate-card research came from. Read each group's rules first: many restrict promotion, and
   a cold link drop reads as spam. Participating and answering questions is what earns the link.
   Note Facebook caches link previews hard – use its Sharing Debugger to force a re-scrape after
   the share image or page metadata changes, or early posts will show a stale card.

## The $0 channel stack for firms (best first)

Everything below is the *demand* side. The first is statewide; the rest (2–6) are our
concentrated hands-on selling, Bay Area first.

1. **Reporter-to-firm referrals** – warmest and most on-model, and the only channel not limited
   by where we can personally reach (built into the product, runs everywhere). A reporter
   vouching to their own attorney client beats any cold outreach.
2. **Rosie's warm network** – her family-law and paralegal contacts, personal intros.
3. **Handwritten postcards** to boutique Bay Area firms – lands on the buyer's desk,
   near-zero cost, doable by two people. See the playbook.
4. **Free/guest attendance** at Bay Area paralegal association meetings (SFPA, East Bay
   PA, PASCCO, SCCoLPA) – aimed right at the buyer. Attend as guests; no sponsorships.
5. **Bar / paralegal listservs + LinkedIn organic** – free presence, Rosie can post.
6. **Cold email** to enriched paralegal contacts at the target firms – last, once warm
   channels are worked.

## Paid acquisition (the next tier, once there's a budget)

Everything above is free. These cost money, so they're **not part of the $0 stack** – they're
where the first real marketing dollars go, once a metro is provably paying and there's budget to
spend. Start small, measure cost per firm signup, kill anything that doesn't convert. Never invent
the numbers – set real caps and read the real results.

1. **Google Search ads (demand side, highest intent).** A paralegal or office manager who types
   "court reporter San Jose," "deposition reporter near me," or "certified shorthand reporter
   California" is a buyer at the moment of need. Point those searches at the directory (or a
   county landing page), not the homepage. Notes:
   - **Intent keywords, tight geo.** Bid on county/city + "court reporter / deposition reporter /
     CSR" terms, geo-fenced to the metro we're actually selling in (Bay Area first, then LA).
     Statewide broad match burns money fast.
   - **Competitor/agency terms** (agency brand names + "alternative") are worth a small, careful
     test – firms shopping an agency are exactly who we want – but watch trademark rules and cost.
   - **Demand, not supply.** Reporters are already free to reach via the claim email + Facebook, so
     don't spend ad money chasing supply. The paid dollar goes to firm search intent.
   - Land on a page that proves the value fast (directory results in their county + the
     agency-vs-DS money comparison) and makes firm signup one click.

2. **ChatGPT / AI-assistant advertising (emerging).** More people now ask an AI assistant "how do
   I find a court reporter in <place>" than a search box. Two plays, and they're different:
   - **Paid placements inside ChatGPT.** OpenAI is rolling out sponsored/ad formats in ChatGPT;
     as they open to smaller advertisers, a sponsored answer to court-reporter / deposition
     queries reaches the buyer inside the tool they're already asking. Treat it like Google
     Search ads – high-intent, geo-targeted, measured on cost per firm signup. This is a newer,
     less-proven surface, so it's a **test-budget line, not a core channel** until the numbers
     earn it.
   - **Be the answer even without paying (answer-engine presence).** Separate and free-ish: make
     the directory the thing an AI *cites* when someone asks how to find a CA court reporter –
     clear public pages, structured/plain descriptions, county coverage the model can read. Worth
     doing regardless of whether we ever buy AI ads; it's the AI-era version of SEO.

**Guardrail on this whole tier:** it breaks the "~$0, two people" rule on purpose, so it waits
until there's money to lose and a metro worth amplifying. Cap spend, attribute every signup, and
compare cost per firm against the free channels before scaling anything.

## What we are deliberately NOT doing (and why)

The v1 plan assumed a ~$2,300 pilot budget and a transaction-fee model. Both are gone,
so these are out:

- **Insurance-carrier "preferred vendor" channel** – relied on cost-savings data and
  volume routing the directory doesn't produce. (Insurance-*defense firms* stay as
  directory targets; the top-down carrier play does not.)
- **Chocolate-box / commissioned-comic mailers, swag, branded business-card rollouts** –
  all budget-dependent. A plain handwritten postcard survives; the deluxe version does not.
- **Law-student ambassador program** – needs paid staff and stipends.
- **Legal-assistant points economy** – funded by a per-order fee that no longer exists.
- **Free Pro months for reporters** – unnecessary; pay-per-invoice already gives them a
  no-commitment reason to join and bring firms.
- **Paid research tools** (Sales Navigator, email-finders) – free manual LinkedIn + a
  Google Sheet instead.

## Guardrails

- Two people, ~$0. If a tactic needs money or staff, it's out or it waits.
- **Supply and reporter-driven demand run statewide; only our manual firm-selling concentrates**
  – Bay Area first, then LA. Don't spread the hands-on selling thin. (See [growth_strategy.md](growth_strategy.md).)
- Pro-reporter-income positioning; never anti-reporter.
- Plain language, sentence case, en-dashes.
