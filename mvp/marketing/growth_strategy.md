# Growth strategy – one page

**The bet:** a statewide directory of California court reporters that reporters keep alive
by bringing their own firms, while we spend our limited hands-on selling only where we can
actually reach. Grow by proving it works in one place, then by geography – not by rebuilding
the old full-featured platform.

## One principle: wide supply, local selling

The directory already covers every licensed CA reporter from day one (it's public data). The
only thing we *concentrate* is the scarce, manual work – signing up paying firms. Everything
automated or reporter-driven runs statewide.

## Two use cases pull in opposite directions

- **Court-audio transcription** – location doesn't matter. A firm anywhere can hire a reporter
  anywhere. Go as wide as we like.
- **Live depos / trials** – the reporter has to be in the room, so it needs reporters *and*
  firms in the same metro. Thin, scattered coverage works nowhere.

So: lead with **transcription** where we don't have local firms yet (it needs no geography),
and lean on **live work** in the metros where reporters and firms both show up.

## Three engines

1. **Directory + reporter claiming – statewide, free.** Every CA reporter is invited to claim.
   Already running. Two ways to reach them: the one-time claim-invite email, and **Facebook,
   which is where CA reporters actually congregate professionally** – our own WC rate-card
   intel came out of a CSR group there. Check each group's rules before posting; many
   professional groups restrict promotion, and a cold link drop from a stranger reads as spam.
   Showing up, answering questions, and being a known name earns the link.
2. **Reporter-brought firms – statewide, free.** A reporter who claims invites the firms she
   already works with, and a small local market seeds itself in her metro without us selling
   there. This is our real statewide engine. The *"invite the firms you already work with"* ask
   has to be loud and central at claim time – hardest, and most important, for reporters outside
   the metros we sell in.
3. **Our direct firm selling – concentrated.** Postcards, Rosie's network, local paralegal
   events. Two people can't run this in 40 metros, so we point it where reporter density and our
   reach overlap: **Bay Area first, LA next.**

## Rough scale (illustrative – CA only, ~5,705 active reporters)

Not a forecast; it's here to react to. Claiming is free; Pro is the flat-monthly reporter
subscription. Prices are unset – dollars live in [pro_pricing_worksheet.md](pro_pricing_worksheet.md).

| If reporters claim | Claimed (free) | On Pro (~5% of all CSRs) | On pay-per-invoice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **10%** | ~570 | ~285 | ~285 |
| **20%** | ~1,140 | ~285 | ~855 |

- **Reporter revenue** = ~285 Pro subscribers × the monthly Pro price, plus the small fee on
  everyone else's invoices.
- **Firms are still the bigger line** (a subscription per paying firm) – the main thing to chase.
- Expanding to another state multiplies the whole base.

## What to do now

1. Run the statewide CSR claim invite; make **"invite your firms"** the central ask, not a footnote.
   Work the CSR **Facebook groups** in parallel – it's the one place reporters are already
   gathered, and it costs nothing but time.
2. Concentrate firm outreach in the **Bay Area** (postcards + Rosie + paralegal events); add **LA**
   once there's reporter density there.
3. **Prove one metro pays.** A handful of paying firms + a working referral loop is the thing that
   decides everything after it.

## What we're not doing

- **Rebuilding the old matching / scheduling / escrow platform** – the cost, complexity, and legal
  risk we cut on purpose. Add a feature only when a *paying* firm asks for that specific thing.
- **Spreading manual firm-selling thin** across the state.
- **Adding states before one metro provably pays.** Expansion is gated on proving one CA metro
  pays; only then do we open a new state (in the order below).

## State expansion order (after one metro pays)

Prioritized by market size, how closely the state mirrors CA's licensed-CSR model (cheaper
directory to stand up), and reciprocity that widens the reporter pool. Don't rebuild v1 to
expand – it's the same directory + reporter-pulled demand, re-pointed at a new state's roster.
Verify licensing specifics per state before launch (see `../docs/state_expansion_checklist.md`).

1. **Texas** – huge market, a similar CSR licensure model, and many freelance deposition
   reporters. The cleanest first expansion (a licensing registry makes the directory cheap to
   build).
2. **Illinois** – 1,400+ licensed reporters, CSR licensure, and an especially strong Chicago
   litigation market.
3. **New Jersey** – a dense legal market wedged between NYC and Philadelphia, with reciprocal
   standards.
4. **Georgia** – Atlanta is a major litigation hub, and Texas recognizes Georgia's certification
   (reciprocity widens the cross-state reporter pool).
5. **Florida** – enormous litigation volume, though its licensing rules differ somewhat from
   Texas, so expect a messier data lift than the states above.
