# Movement positioning and partner strategy

Rosie's framing (2026-07-29): Dewey Sholes isn't just a directory – it's a push to (a) let
independent court reporters keep more of what they earn, and (b) reduce how much of a litigant's
money gets absorbed by a middleman on the way to a transcript. This doc turns that into usable
copy, a tagline shortlist, a plan for the "money saved vs. an agency" dashboard metric already
scoped in [`../docs/roadmap.md`](../docs/roadmap.md), and a researched partner/cross-promotion
shortlist – including a direct answer to "is there a court reporters' union."

Companion docs: [`README.md`](README.md) (channel index) · [`growth_strategy.md`](growth_strategy.md)
(the sequencing) · [`../../research/agency_invoice_evidence.md`](../../research/agency_invoice_evidence.md)
(the real invoice math this whole narrative rests on) · [`../docs/roadmap.md`](../docs/roadmap.md)
(the dashboard metric spec).

## 1. Movement / hero positioning

### The core positioning statement

**Dewey Sholes exists because a court reporter's invoice has two very different stories on it: what
the reporter did, and what the agency added on top. We built a way for the person who did the work
and the person who's paying for it to deal with each other directly – so less of the money in
between goes to a markup neither of them asked for.**

That's the whole thesis. Two real, anonymized CA invoices back it up (not a guess): on one, 39% of
the bill was agency line items the reporter never touched; on a remote deposition, it was 70–82%.
See [`agency_invoice_evidence.md`](../../research/agency_invoice_evidence.md) for both. We use the
30–50% range (or the specific 39% figure from the arbitration invoice) – never the retired 11%/12%
figures, which described an abandoned pricing model.

### Why this matters – reporters

Court reporting in California has been squeezed from two directions at once: state budget cuts
pushed staff reporters out of the courtroom starting around 2008 (LA and San Diego Superior Courts
laid off dozens of staff reporters; California's judicial branch absorbed over a billion dollars in
cuts – [LAist](https://laist.com/news/kpcc-archive/court-reporters-disappear-courtrooms-because-budge),
[CBS SF](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/deep-california-budget-cuts-force-litigants-to-pay-for-court-reporters/)),
and the agencies that absorbed the resulting private-reporting demand built a business model on
markups the reporter never sees. A working CSR put it plainly in a public Facebook post that's
become one of our clearest pieces of evidence: *"The agencies overcharging like this is why the
attorneys are turning on us, saying we're making too much... Tell attorneys what your charges are."*
(full quote in [`agency_invoice_evidence.md`](../../research/agency_invoice_evidence.md)).

**The reporter-facing narrative:** *You didn't choose to become a freelancer competing against
agency markups – the system pushed you there. Dewey Sholes doesn't ask you to take on more risk.
It removes the markup that was never yours in the first place. Your rate is your rate. The attorney
sees it before they confirm. You keep more of what you bill.*

**The core defensive angle – this may be the most resonant message of all, so it gets its own
paragraph.** Court reporters' single biggest fear is losing the work entirely to electronic/digital
recording, and that fear has real teeth right now: California's AB 3013 pilot put remote court
reporting (not electronic recording) in 13 counties starting July 2025, but separate bills this
session (AB 882, AB 1189) address expanding electronic recording itself, and the CA Access to
Justice Commission's own numbers show 72.4% of unlimited-civil, family, and probate hearings ran
with no verbatim record at all from January–March 2026
([courts.ca.gov shortage page](https://courts.ca.gov/news-reference/research-data/shortage-court-reporters-california),
[Digital Democracy on AB 3013](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202320240ab3013)).
Cost is a direct driver of that shift, not a side issue: a private court reporter runs roughly
$3,300/day for a trial or $2,580/day for a deposition by the Judicial Council's own figures
([courtscribes.com](https://courtscribes.com/how-much-does-a-certified-court-reporter-cost-in-2026/)),
and that price has already pushed litigants to ask courts for electronic/AI alternatives instead –
one documented case had a party seeking to replace a human stenographer with video + AI
transcription specifically to cut a deposition's cost from about $4,000 to $1,500
([Harvard tag team / court order coverage](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/3624/feed_items/14629622)).
**The honest, provable claim: a meaningful share of that inflated cost is agency markup, not the
reporter's rate – so cutting the markup helps keep human reporting price-competitive with the
recording alternative that would otherwise replace it.** That's not "DS saves jobs" (we can't prove
that on our own) – it's "DS removes a cost penalty that currently pushes the market toward
recording instead of a human reporter," which is a claim the invoice evidence actually supports.

### Why this matters – firms (and, indirectly, litigants)

**Precision matters here – Rosie is a lawyer, and this claim has to survive a lawyer's read.** The
honest chain is: agencies mark up transcripts by 30–50% on line items the reporter never performed
→ a firm using Dewey Sholes pays a fair reporter rate plus a modest platform fee instead of that
markup → **whether that specific saving reaches the litigant depends on how the firm bills its
client.** We do not control that, and we should never claim it as a guarantee.

**The correct framing:** *less of a litigant's money goes to a middleman that did none of the
actual work.* Not "your client will pay less" – we don't know that for any given case. Say **"helps
bring down the cost of getting to trial"** or **"less markup between the work and the bill,"** never
"your litigant saves $X."

**The firm-facing narrative:** *Attorney fees already include court-reporting costs, and those costs
often carry an agency markup that has nothing to do with the reporter's skill or the transcript's
accuracy. Dewey Sholes lets you deal with the reporter directly – see their real rate before you
confirm, pay it, done. Less of what your client is ultimately billed for goes to a markup that
served no one but the agency.*

### Values / manifesto blurb (About page, footer, or a dedicated "why we exist" section)

> Court reporting has a shortage problem and a middleman problem, and they're related. Reporters
> have been pushed out of stable courtroom jobs and into freelance work by budget cuts they didn't
> choose – and then agencies built a business on marking up that freelance work by 30 to 50 percent,
> in line items the reporter never touches. That markup doesn't just cost the reporter money – it's
> part of why hiring a human reporter has gotten expensive enough that courts and litigants are
> turning to electronic recording instead. We think the person who does the work should keep what
> they earn, the person paying for a transcript should be able to see exactly where the money goes,
> and a licensed human reporter should be able to compete on price with a recording device instead
> of losing the job to one. Dewey Sholes is a directory, a way to message directly, and a way to get
> paid – built so less money sits between the two of you, and so human court reporting can stay in
> the room.

Keep this short-form, sentence case, en-dashes, no "moat/flywheel/movement-as-buzzword" phrasing on
customer-facing copy (see the tagline notes below on the word "movement" itself).

## 2. Tagline / CTA options

Aud's line – *"Don't just sign up for our platform, help make this work we do better for the
workers and the injured parties"* – is the right instinct: recruit people as participants in
fixing something, not as customers of a product. Ranked below, with fit and rationale. All are
draft options, not decided copy – pick, combine, or reject freely.

| # | Line | Fits | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Cut the markup, not the reporter."** | Both, and doubles as reporter/union outreach | Shortest, cleanest summary of the whole thesis – and the two-sided pun (don't cut the markup *or* the reporter's job) directly answers reporters' biggest fear: losing the work to electronic recording. Works as a hero line, a one-liner in cold outreach, and a union-outreach opener. Doesn't overclaim the litigant angle. Promoted to #1 after the electronic-recording research below. |
| 2 | **"Make the human reporter the affordable option again."** | Reporters and union/official-reporter outreach primary | New, directly answers the "we're losing jobs to recording" fear with a claim the invoice evidence actually supports: agency markup is part of why human reporting got priced out, and cutting it helps a licensed human compete with a recording device on cost. Careful wording matters – "the affordable option again," not "we save your job," keeps it honest (helps/removes a cost penalty, doesn't guarantee outcomes). |
| 3 | **"See the rate. Skip the markup."** | Both | Ties directly to the real match-preview / rate-visibility feature (attorneys see the reporter's actual rate before confirming – a genuine, provable differentiator, not a slogan). |
| 4 | **"Built so the person who does the work keeps what they earn."** | Reporters (leads), works for both | Closest match to Rosie's framing #1 (empower reporters). Warm, not salesy. Good About-page line. |
| 5 | **"Help us keep more of the money with the people who did the work."** | Both | Direct descendant of Aud's floated line – "help us" makes the signup an act of participation, not a purchase. Slightly long for a hero but strong as a CTA button subhead or email close. |
| 6 | **"Your invoice should say what actually happened. Now it can."** | Firms (leads), works for reporters too | Plain, concrete, ties to the real invoice evidence without needing to cite a stat inline. Good for cold email subject lines. |
| 7 | **"Less to the agency. More to the record."** | Both | Compact, a little more poetic – "the record" nods to the court-reporting craft itself, not just money. Risk: "the record" may need one line of context on first use. |
| 8 | **"Every job here is one less dollar going somewhere it shouldn't."** | Both, best for reporter recruitment emails | Participatory framing – casts using DS as a small act with a cumulative effect, which sets up the impact-metric tie-in in §3 nicely. Slightly long; trim for a hero. |
| 9 | **"We're not an agency. That's the whole idea."** | Both | Punchy, contrarian, good for ad copy or a landing-page subhead aimed at firms who already use an agency. Doesn't carry the "empower reporters" half on its own – pair with supporting copy. |
| 10 | **"Sign up. Then help us make it fairer for the next reporter and the next client."** | Both, best as a post-signup or onboarding message rather than a hero | Most literal execution of Aud's original line (recruit as participant). Works better as an in-product moment (e.g., after claiming a profile, or after a firm's first paid invoice) than as top-of-funnel copy – it presumes the visitor already gets the mission. |
| 11 | **"A fairer cut, built by the people who felt the unfair one."** | Reporters primary | Leans hardest into "movement" energy. Riskiest of the eleven – "felt the unfair one" is a little abstract for a first-time visitor with no context, and could read as adversarial toward agencies in a way that needs careful placement (not the very first thing a new agency-affiliated reporter reads). Save for reporter-specific channels (the CSR Facebook groups, the claim-invite email) where the audience already shares the frustration.

**On #2 specifically – never overclaim.** "Helps make human reporting more affordable relative to
electronic recording" is a claim the invoice/cost evidence supports (see §1's "core defensive
angle" and the sourcing there). "Dewey Sholes saves reporter jobs" is not something we can prove
and should never be said as a guarantee – always frame as *helps*, *removes a cost penalty*, or
*makes it more possible*, never as a promise of outcome.

**One flag on the word "movement" itself:** it's the right internal frame (this doc's title uses
it), but the literal word reads as startup-jargon-adjacent on customer-facing copy – it's vague
until backed by specifics, and the plain-language rule pushes toward showing the mechanism (the
rate, the invoice, the markup) rather than naming the abstraction. Use "movement" in strategy docs
and maybe press/earned-media language; keep site copy concrete (taglines above), not "join the
movement."

## 3. Hero/impact metric tie-in – the "money saved vs. an agency" dashboard number

`../docs/roadmap.md` already scopes this stat (added 2026-07-29, Jay/Rosie's "make it sticky"
advice) – a dashboard number for both sides showing money kept out of a middleman's hands. This is
the load-bearing connection between the movement narrative and something a user actually sees every
time they log in – exactly the role Too Good To Go's "meals saved" counter plays for them.

### Why it works as the "receipt"

A mission statement is easy to skim past. A number that changes every time someone gets paid or
pays an invoice is not. Too Good To Go doesn't tell users they're heroes – it shows them a
running count of meals they personally saved, and lets the user do the feeling-good math
themselves. The DS equivalent is the same move: don't say "you're helping fix the industry," show
"here's what avoiding the agency markup was actually worth this month," and let the number carry
the message.

### How to keep it honest (binding – see [[feedback_never_invent_data]])

- **Always an estimate, always labeled as one**, with the basis shown inline: *"est. vs. a typical
  ~39% agency markup, from a real CA invoice"* – linking or footnoting
  [`agency_invoice_evidence.md`](../../research/agency_invoice_evidence.md).
- **Use the current pricing model** (reporter pays 2–5% to get paid; firm pays a subscription, no
  transaction fee) – the "amount kept" math is against the agency's cut, not against DS's own fee,
  and it should reflect the real fee structure, not the retired 12%.
- **Don't double-count.** The reporter's "cut you kept" and the firm's "markup avoided" describe the
  *same* dollars from two sides of one transaction – never imply the platform "created" savings
  twice by adding the two views together in any copy or aggregate figure.
- **Needs real transactions.** Until Stripe is live, this is illustrative/demo data in the
  prototype, clearly flagged as such – never presented as a live figure before there's a live
  payment behind it.

### Per-user surfacing (once payments are real)

- A StatCard on the reporter Overview tab ("cut you kept this month/year") and the firm dashboard
  ("agency markup avoided"), per the roadmap spec.
- A lifetime running total once a user has enough history – this is the closest DS equivalent of
  Too Good To Go's "meals saved since you joined," and it rewards long-term use rather than any
  single transaction.
- Keep the same real invoice/number the homepage `MoneyComparison` already uses, so the story is
  identical from first landing-page visit through daily dashboard use – one number, not two
  competing claims.

### Later: an aggregate community figure

Once there's enough real transaction volume to compute one honestly, a single community-wide
number ("$X kept from agencies across every Dewey Sholes reporter and firm") is a strong earned-
media and homepage hook – but it has real preconditions:

- It needs actual aggregate transaction data, not an extrapolation from a handful of early users.
- It should probably show a date range or "since launch" framing so it doesn't read as inflated
  once growth slows.
- Same non-negotiable labeling as the per-user version: state the agency-markup benchmark it's
  measured against, and don't invent a number to fill the space before we have one. Until then, the
  invoice evidence and industry-level statistics (agency take-rate range, the court-reporter
  shortage stats in §4A/§4F below) are the only numbers we cite – real, sourced, not ours.

## 4. Partner / cross-promotion research

Everything below was verified by web search on 2026-07-29 and is cited. Where a source didn't give
a clean fact (e.g., current membership counts), that's stated as unknown rather than estimated.
Prioritized CA first (launch market), then Texas (first expansion). This is a vetted shortlist, not
an exhaustive directory – deeper profiles can go in `marketing/competitive-analysis/`-style entries
later if a specific partnership moves forward.

### Guardrails before any outreach (read this before contacting anyone below)

Rosie flagged a real risk (2026-07-29): official/union court reporters are in a genuinely anxious,
defensive position. Many were pushed out of stable, unionized courtroom jobs by budget cuts starting
around 2008 and forced into freelance or agency work to make a living – so this audience reads any
further change to the status quo as a potential new threat, not automatically as help. Two things
follow:

1. **DS can honestly stand with them, not against them.** The precarious freelance/agency reality
   they were pushed into is exactly what DS improves – it helps freelance reporters keep more of
   what they bill and cuts the agency's 30–50% cut. That's a real, provable alliance, not spin.
2. **DS must not read as "part of the problem."** Concretely, that means:
   - **Champion licensed human court reporters, always.** DS is a directory of licensed CSRs, not
     an electronic-recording or AI-transcription product. Never let outreach copy blur that line –
     replacing human stenographers is the single biggest fear in this community (see §4A/§4F), and
     DS's actual model (human reporters, transparent rates) is the opposite of that threat.
   - **Lead with the jobs angle, not just the money angle, when talking to this audience.** The
     honest chain: agency markup inflates the cost of hiring a human reporter (Judicial Council
     figures put a private reporter at roughly $3,300/day for a trial, $2,580/day for a deposition –
     [courtscribes.com](https://courtscribes.com/how-much-does-a-certified-court-reporter-cost-in-2026/)),
     that cost is a real reason courts and litigants turn to electronic or AI recording instead (one
     documented case sought to cut a deposition from about $4,000 to $1,500 by swapping a
     stenographer for video + AI –
     [Harvard tag team](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/3624/feed_items/14629622)), and CA
     currently has active legislation on both fronts (AB 3013's remote-reporting pilot in 13
     counties since July 2025; AB 882 and AB 1189 addressing electronic recording –
     [Digital Democracy](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202320240ab3013)). Cutting
     the markup helps keep a human reporter price-competitive with the recording alternative that
     would otherwise take the work. Say it as *"helps keep human reporting affordable enough to
     stay the default"* – never as a guarantee that any specific job or position is saved.
   - **Frame DS as fixing gig precarity, not adding to it.** The pitch is "keep more of what you
     already bill," not "here's more flexible/on-demand work" – the second framing reads as more
     gig-economy commoditization, which is the wrong note for this audience.
   - **Don't position against unions or official-reporter jobs.** DS has no stake in courtroom
     staffing policy; stay out of that fight entirely rather than look like a side in it.
   - **Lead outreach with empathy, not a signup pitch.** "We know reporters were pushed into
     freelance work that didn't used to exist, and we're trying to make that world fairer" comes
     before any mention of the product.

### 4A. Is there a court reporters' union? – the direct answer

**Yes and no, and the distinction matters for how DS should talk to each group – but it's a
spectrum, not two separate camps, because many reporters work both sides of the line.**

**Freelance/deposition reporters – DS's core supply – are independent contractors and are not
unionized.** Instead of a union, they're represented by trade associations:
- **DRA / CalDRA (Deposition Reporters Association of California)** – by its own description, "the
  nation's largest trade association dedicated to the freelance deposition reporter," founded in
  1995 specifically because agencies were offering services/pricing to one side of litigation but
  not the other – a founding mission almost identical to DS's transparency pitch
  ([caldra.org](https://www.caldra.org/), [cal-ccra.org](https://www.cal-ccra.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=59)).
- **CCRA (California Court Reporters Association)** – broader membership: freelance, official,
  CART/captioning, students, **and firm owners** ([cal-ccra.org](https://www.cal-ccra.org/)).
- **NCRA (National Court Reporters Association)** – the national body, 12,000+ members across
  freelance, official, and captioning roles, with resources aimed specifically at independent
  contractors ([ncra.org](https://www.ncra.org/home/about-ncra)).

**Official (court-employed) reporters are unionized – as public-sector employees, county by
county** – but many also take per diem/pro tem or private freelance assignments on the side, so
this is not a hard line between "union people" and "our people":
- **San Francisco** – SFOCRA (San Francisco Official Court Reporters Association), affiliated with
  **IFPTE Local 21**
  ([ifpte21.org](https://ifpte21.org/chapters/sf-superior-court)).
- **Los Angeles** – **SEIU Local 721**
  ([seiu721.org](https://www.seiu721.org/la-county-court-reporter-headquarters.php)).
- **San Diego** – **SEIU Local 221**
  ([seiu221.org](https://www.seiu221.org/court-reporters-fcca/)).
- **Alameda** – the Alameda County Official Court Reporters Association (ACOCRA); some CA counties
  also list court-reporter classifications under **AFSCME** bargaining units per job postings.

**Why the overlap matters more than the split:** California courts cut hundreds of staff-reporter
positions starting around 2008 (LA and San Diego Superior Courts each laid off dozens; the judicial
branch absorbed over $1B in cuts statewide –
[LAist](https://laist.com/news/kpcc-archive/court-reporters-disappear-courtrooms-because-budge),
[CBS SF](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/deep-california-budget-cuts-force-litigants-to-pay-for-court-reporters/)).
Courts also lean on **pro tem/per diem reporters** to cover gaps
([courts.ca.gov fact sheet](https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/file/court-reporter-fact-sheet-jan-2025.pdf)),
and there's direct evidence of reporters leaving staff jobs for private freelance work over working
conditions even without layoffs (Contra Costa lost 10 staff reporters 2022–2024 –
found via search of courts.ca.gov reporting). So a union member today may also be a DS user
tomorrow for their side freelance work – the "help the people pushed into freelance work keep more
of what they earn" story covers the full spectrum, not just the reporters who left staff jobs
entirely.

### 4B. Launch outreach to the unions/official-reporter associations – a concrete plan

Aud wants to approach these groups at launch. Given the guardrails above, the realistic ask is
**alliance and visibility, not a formal endorsement** – a public-sector union formally endorsing a
for-profit product is an unusual and probably unwelcome ask, and risks reading as exactly the kind
of overreach the guardrails warn against.

**What to actually ask for (in rough order of how much to expect):**
1. **A courtesy briefing, not a pitch.** Reach out (SFOCRA/IFPTE Local 21 first – Rosie's original
   lead; then SEIU 721 in LA, SEIU 221 in San Diego) with the empathy-first framing, leading with
   the jobs angle, not the money angle: we know reporters have been pushed into freelance/per diem
   work by budget cuts, we know the profession's biggest fear is losing more work to electronic
   recording, and we built something that cuts the agency markup that currently makes a human
   reporter more expensive than the recording alternative – which helps freelance/per diem work
   (including their own members' side work) stay price-competitive. That's the opener, before any
   mention of signing up.
2. **A mention in member communications**, if they're willing – not "endorse Dewey Sholes," but
   "here's a resource that helps freelance/per diem reporting pay more fairly and stay affordable
   against electronic recording," which also reinforces their own broader argument that stenography
   is a viable profession worth investing in (useful to them in the electronic-recording fight, see
   §4F).
3. **A standing invite to any CSR-facing DS event or webinar** as a guest voice on the
   official-reporter perspective – low-commitment, no product ask attached.
4. **Do not ask for a public endorsement or co-branding.** It's the wrong shape of ask for a
   public-sector union and the union may not have a mechanism to grant it anyway.

**Conflict to watch:** none of these unions or CCRA/DRA should ever hear DS described in a way that
sounds like electronic recording, AI transcription, or "flexible gig work" – see the guardrails
above. Frame every touchpoint around licensed human reporters keeping more of what they bill.

### 4C. Trade associations – the natural first partners (CA)

- **DRA/CalDRA** is the closest values match: founded to fight exactly the agency-favoritism/
  transparency problem DS's whole model addresses. Good targets: a mention or short piece in
  *The Deposition Reporter* newsletter, attending/sponsoring (as budget allows – likely just
  attending, per the $0 constraint) their events, and specifically reaching out about a shared
  interest in transparency once DS has real transaction data to point to.
- **CCRA** reaches a wider audience (official + freelance + CART + students) but **its membership
  includes firm/agency owners** – some of whom are the businesses DS's pitch positions against.
  Engage CCRA on shared professional-standards ground (education, the shortage crisis, defending
  human stenography against electronic recording) rather than leading with the "cut the agency"
  message in that specific channel.
- **NCRA** (national) is the right long-horizon relationship for Texas/Illinois expansion and for
  answer-engine/press credibility, but it's broad and slower-moving than the CA-specific groups.

### 4D. Steno / court reporting schools (supply pipeline)

The shortage is real and documented: CA court reporting schools dropped from 17 to 8 since 2010,
only 176 new licenses were issued statewide in FY2024–25, and the CCRR skills-exam pass rate across
the last three sittings was about 53% ([research.com](https://research.com/rankings/trade-schools/court-reporter/best-court-reporter-trade-schools-in-california),
[steno.com/brief](https://brief.steno.com/californias-court-reporter-shortage-is-leaving-millions-of-hearings-off-the-record)).
CCRA maintains a list of CA court reporting schools at
[cal-ccra.org/court-reporting-schools](https://www.cal-ccra.org/court-reporting-schools) – that's
the right starting roster rather than us naming specific schools we haven't independently verified.

**Cross-promo idea:** a light-touch relationship with a handful of CA programs – guest a
"what happens after you're certified" session on freelance income/DS, or sponsor a small student
prize – gives DS visibility with the exact new-CSR cohort that has no agency relationships yet and
is deciding how to build their client base from day one. Low cost, long payoff, no conflict with
the guardrails (this is pro-profession, not pro-replacing-reporters).

### 4E. Independent-worker / freelancer advocacy (secondary, not reporter-specific)

**Freelancers Union** (national, NY-based) advocates broadly for independent workers – benefits,
policy, and was cited as an active voice in California's freelancer-protection legislation
([KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12019477/freelancing-in-california-know-your-new-rights-in-2025)).
Not reporter-specific, so it's a secondary/optional channel – a blog guest post or resource-listing
mention could reinforce the "empower independent contractors" half of the mission narrative to an
audience outside court reporting, but it shouldn't be a priority ahead of the reporter-specific
associations above.

### 4F. Access-to-justice / legal aid – real narrative, but a genuine conflict to flag

The "court reporter shortage as an access-to-justice crisis" framing is real and well documented:
the CA Access to Justice Commission and Bay Area Legal Aid cite over a million hearings in family,
probate, and civil cases with no verbatim record from March 2023–March 2024
([baylegal.org](https://baylegal.org/court-reporter-shortage-faq/)). **But their proposed fix is
expanding electronic recording in courtrooms** – Bay Area Legal Aid and the Family Violence
Appellate Project petitioned the CA Supreme Court for it, and CCRA, DRA, and SEIU (the same groups
in §4A–4C) publicly opposed that petition
([Capitol Weekly](https://capitolweekly.net/court-reporters-and-the-new-front-in-the-war-over-electronic-recording/)).

**Recommendation: cite the shortage/cost statistics for narrative color (they're real and
sourced), but do not pursue a formal partnership with Bay Area Legal Aid or the CA Access to
Justice Commission.** Their solution (recording devices replacing human reporters) is the exact
thing the guardrails in this doc warn DS away from being associated with. DS's actual position
threads a genuinely different needle – cheaper access through *fairly priced human reporters*, not
through replacing them – which is more aligned with CCRA/DRA than with Legal Aid's own proposal.
Don't publicly take a side in that legislative fight either way; just don't seek this specific
partnership.

### 4G. Women-owned business networks

Dewey Sholes is women-owned (Aud). Relevant, mostly free/low-cost networks:
- **WBENC** certification runs through regional partners – **WBEC-Pacific** covers Northern
  California/the Bay Area (where DS is based); **WBEC-West** covers Southern California/LA
  ([wbec-pacific.org](https://wbec-pacific.org/certification/)). Note: WBENC certification
  typically involves a real review process and a fee – flag as a possible future cost item, not a
  $0 channel, before pursuing it.
- **NAWBO** (National Association of Women Business Owners) has regional chapters (e.g., NAWBO
  Orange County) that run free/low-cost networking events and member features – a more realistic
  $0-budget entry point than formal WBENC certification.

**Cross-promo idea:** local NAWBO chapter events and newsletter mentions are a reasonable earned-
visibility channel and don't require the WBENC certification cost to start.

### 4H. County bar associations, paralegal groups, solo/small-firm networks (demand side)

Already covered in [`README.md`](README.md) (SFPA, East Bay PA, PASCCO, SCCoLPA paralegal
associations) – no new research needed there. One addition worth flagging: most CA county bar
associations run a **solo/small-firm section** (e.g., the Bar Association of San Francisco, Alameda
County Bar Association, Santa Clara County Bar Association) which is a natural, low-cost demand-side
audience alongside the paralegal associations already in the plan – but specific chapter contacts
and event calendars still need verifying before outreach, same as the existing firm playbook.

### 4I. Texas (first expansion) – one thing to flag now, not act on yet

**MyTexasCSR** (mytexascsr.com) is a job-marketplace run jointly by **TCRA** (Texas Court Reporters
Association, machine writers) and **TEXDRA** (Texas Deposition Reporters Association, freelance,
both voice and machine writers) – open to any TX reporter, no association membership required
([texdra.org](https://www.texdra.org/who-we-are.html), site content via direct fetch). This is a
genuine complication for the "Texas first" expansion plan: the two trade associations that would
otherwise be DS's natural TX-launch partners (mirroring CCRA/DRA in CA) already co-own a competing
product. Full write-up now lives as a stub at
[`marketing/competitive-analysis/mytexascsr.md`](../../marketing/competitive-analysis/mytexascsr.md)
(added to the README's "watching" tier 2026-07-29) – worth a real look, and a direct conversation
with TCRA/TEXDRA about whether DS complements or competes with it, before Texas expansion moves from
plan to action.
