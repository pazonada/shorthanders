## What this doc is (and isn't)

This doc captures growth and product-expansion ideas that are explicitly **out of MVP scope** but are worth holding onto for post-launch evaluation. Anything here is one of:
- A market-expansion idea (new product surface, new customer segment, new geography)
- A new revenue line we'd add on top of the core marketplace
- A platform-extension idea that depends on us first hitting some MVP milestone

Anything in MVP scope should be captured in `marketing_strategy_master.md`, the relevant phase plan, the PRD, or a dedicated procedure doc — not here. If an idea here graduates to "we're actually doing this," it gets moved into a real plan and removed from this holding pen.

## Idea 1: Court-runner / courier service (Rosie, 2026-05-09)

**The pain:** Today, law firms pay roughly **$100 to dispatch a "court runner" (courier)** to physically pick up a CD or paper transcript from a courthouse. This is a recurring cost that firms grumble about — the courier itself isn't doing skilled work, but the firm needs the physical handoff.

**The idea:** Once Dewey Sholes has volume, we develop relationships with court-runner services and bulk-purchase pickups. We then offer firms courier pickup as an add-on at a **lower price than $100** — perhaps $40-60 — wrapped into the order. Best fit for non-rush jobs where we can batch pickups.

**Why this could work:**
- Real, recurring pain point firms already pay for.
- Bulk discount is a normal procurement play — no innovation required.
- Margin opportunity (we keep the spread between bulk rate and what we charge the firm).
- Differentiator vs. agencies that don't handle courier logistics.
- Could be a separate line item or bundled into "white-glove" pricing tiers.

**Why this is post-MVP:**
- We have to *first* prove transcript-marketplace fit, then add an adjacent service.
- Bulk-pickup relationships require negotiating with multiple courier services in multiple metros — a real biz-dev effort.
- Most pilot jobs will be digital delivery anyway; physical CD pickups are a subset of cases where the courthouse hasn't moved to electronic delivery.

**Open questions to revisit later:**
- Do we partner with existing court-runner services or build our own dispatch?
- Per-pickup pricing model vs. monthly subscription for high-volume firms?
- Geographic coverage — likely starts in metros where we have firm density.

**Trigger condition for revisiting:** ≥ 50 active firms on the platform AND consistent feedback that physical pickup is a meaningful operational cost they'd pay us to absorb.

## Idea 2: Deposition transcription expansion (Rosie, ongoing discussion)

**Current scope:** MVP is **court proceedings only** — see `governance-and-process/scope_public_court_proceedings_only.md`. Depositions are explicitly out of scope.

**The idea:** Open the platform to **deposition transcription** as a separate workflow. Massive market expansion — depositions outnumber court proceedings by a wide margin in most civil practices.

**Why this is currently out of scope (recap):**
- Different legal/regulatory profile. Depositions involve attorney-client privilege, work-product, and confidentiality stakes that public court proceedings do not.
- Different operational profile. Depositions are scheduled by attorneys at private locations (firm conference rooms, court reporter offices, video-conference) — not at courthouses.
- Different competitive landscape. Deposition transcription is dominated by entrenched agencies (Veritext, Esquire, U.S. Legal Support) with deep firm relationships and complex scheduling/exhibit-handling tooling.
- Different liability profile. We'd need to handle deposition exhibits, witness identification, errata sheets, and the full deposition-record workflow.

**Why this could still be the right next move:**
- Depositions are where the transcript volume and revenue actually live in civil practice.
- The same marketplace mechanics (CSR matching, transparent pricing, no agency markup) apply.
- CSRs covering depositions are largely the same population as CSRs covering court hearings.
- Once the platform has CSR supply and firm trust, deposition is the natural expansion.

**Open questions to revisit:**
- Liability: do we need a different insurance posture for depositions vs. court proceedings? (Probably yes — exhibit handling and confidentiality stakes are higher.)
- Scheduling: depositions need calendar coordination among multiple parties (witness, counsel for each side, CSR, possibly videographer). Court proceedings already have a fixed time and place.
- Confidentiality / data-retention: deposition transcripts often have sealed portions, protective orders, and longer retention requirements.
- Do we add deposition support inside Dewey Sholes, or spin up a sibling product?

**Trigger condition for revisiting:** Pilot has demonstrated marketplace fit (≥ 50 firms, ≥ 200 active CSRs, sustained order volume) AND we have legal sign-off on the additional liability exposure.
