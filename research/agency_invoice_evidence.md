# Agency invoice evidence: a real-world CSR rate-card frustration

**Captured:** 2026-05-13
**Source:** Public post in "Court Reporter Rates Discussion" Facebook group (11K members), screenshot shared by Aud.
**Why it's here:** Concrete proof that (a) agency markups consume a meaningful share of attorney invoices, (b) CSRs themselves see this as the core injustice in their industry, and (c) DS's marketplace structure directly addresses what CSRs are publicly demanding.

---

## The invoice

A deposition transcript bill issued by a CSR agency to a law firm. Case reference: AAA No. 01-23-0001-1770 (American Arbitration Association arbitration, 2023 case). Deposition of Jon Stephens, 319-page transcript.

| Line item | Unit price | Quantity | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Administrative/Account Service Fee | $210.14 | 1 | $210.14 |
| Electronic Delivery | $25.00 | 1 | $25.00 |
| Exhibit Tabs | $0.50 | 28 | $14.00 |
| Exhibits – Black and White | $0.55 | 143 | $78.65 |
| Exhibits – Color | $1.15 | 8 | $9.20 |
| Production Fee | $150.00 | 1 | $150.00 |
| Copy of Transcript | $5.50 | 319 | $1,754.50 |
| E-Bundle (Under 200 Pages) | $70.00 | 1 | $70.00 |
| **Amount Due** | | | **$2,311.49** |
| After 30 Days (late fee) | | | $231.15 |
| **Balance Due if late** | | | **$2,542.64** |

## Who actually does each line of work

Splitting the invoice by who performs the labor:

| Line item | Performed by | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Copy of Transcript ($5.50 × 319 pgs) | **CSR** – stenography, scoping, proofreading, certifying | $1,754.50 |
| Administrative/Account Service Fee | Agency overhead (billing, scheduling, customer service) | $210.14 |
| Production Fee | Agency assembly (cover page, certificate, errata) | $150.00 |
| E-Bundle | Agency packaging (compiling files into a deliverable) | $70.00 |
| Exhibit Tabs + B&W + Color | Agency physical-handling (scanning, indexing exhibits) | $101.85 |
| Electronic Delivery | Agency emails the PDF | $25.00 |
| **Pure agency markup (non-CSR work)** | | **$556.99** |

**24% of the invoice is line items the CSR never touches.**

## What the CSR actually nets

Using the Atkinson-Baker reporter-payout rate sheet (CA agency, public via screenshot — see [pricing_questions_rosie_and_csrs.md](pricing_questions_rosie_and_csrs.md) research summary): the agency pays its CSRs **$4.40/page for hearings, trials, and arbitrations**. Personal-injury deposition rate is $3.00/page; this is an arbitration so we use $4.40.

| | Amount | Share of invoice |
|---|---|---|
| CSR earns (319 × $4.40) | $1,403.60 | 61% |
| Agency margin on page rate (319 × $1.10) | $350.90 | 15% |
| Agency line-item extras | $556.99 | 24% |
| **Total agency take** | **$907.89** | **39%** |

The CSR keeps less than two-thirds of the invoice. **Roughly 4 out of every 10 dollars the attorney paid for this transcript went to the agency, not to the person who actually produced it.**

This sits in the middle of the 30–50% agency-take range CSRs have estimated anecdotally and roughly matches what the rate-card research suggested. We can now cite a specific data point.

## The CSR's reaction — verbatim

From the top comment on the Facebook post, by Caldarella Cassandra (a working CSR with a published rate sheet of her own — see prior rate-card research):

> The agencies overcharging like this is why the attorneys are turning on us, saying we're making too much. What attorneys don't realize is that we're held to statutory rates and we're working with 50-year old rates without having gotten the cost of living increases for 50 years, but the agencies can do this without a license or repercussions. The agencies are destroying our industry and are putting us all out of a job. We must stop this before it's too late. Tell attorneys what your charges are. Send attorneys your invoices so they can see that reporters are NOT the ones overcharging. Advocate for transparency laws in your states where agencies have to reveal their costs, like they do in the medical industry now. We can't let stenographers go by way of the dinosaurs and become extinct.

## Caveats worth flagging

- **The exact CSR take-home is estimated**, not directly observed. The $4.40/page rate comes from Atkinson-Baker; this invoice may be from a different agency with different reporter payouts. The 39% agency-take figure could be ±5–10 percentage points.
- **Exhibit handling is real work** that someone has to do. On DS, either the CSR does it (and bills for it) or the firm handles its own exhibits. The "$50 estimated" in the DS comparison is a placeholder — we should decide explicitly whether DS supports a la carte exhibit handling or whether the firm handles its own (and if the latter, surface that as a feature, not a gap).
- **Production Fee, E-Bundle, Electronic Delivery** are largely software-substitutable. On DS, these are essentially "we generated and emailed a PDF" — zero marginal cost. That's where the 24% non-CSR markup disappears, not because agencies are evil but because we have software they don't.
- **This is one invoice.** Pattern claims need more data points. If Rosie or her network can supply 2–3 more anonymized invoices, we can publish ranges rather than a single anecdote.
