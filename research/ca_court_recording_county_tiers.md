## Statutory baseline

Per **Gov. Code § 69957**, all 58 California counties can use electronic recording, but only in **limited civil, misdemeanor, and infraction proceedings**, and only when a court reporter is unavailable.

**Family law, probate, and unlimited civil are statutorily off-limits** for electronic recording absent a separate court order.

A small number of counties have issued general orders expanding ER beyond this statutory scope (see "Tier A" below). A pending lawsuit (FVAP / Bay Area Legal Aid v. Contra Costa, LA, Santa Clara, San Diego, filed Dec 2024) is pushing for a statewide rule. Expect the list of expanded-ER counties to grow over the next 12–24 months.

## Remote court reporting pilot (different from ER – do not confuse)

A separate Judicial-Council remote-CSR pilot launched July 2025 in 13 counties. This is a logistics workaround (CSR appears remotely via video), not an authorization to record audio in place of a CSR.

Pilot counties: **Alameda, Contra Costa, Los Angeles, Mendocino, Monterey, Orange, San Bernardino, San Diego, San Joaquin, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Tulare, Ventura.**

The strategic implication for Shorthanders: in these 13 counties the courts have already accepted the principle that the reporter doesn't have to be physically present, which makes remote-CSR booking via our marketplace politically easier than in counties that haven't run the pilot.

### Judicial Council framing (the floor, not the ceiling)

The Judicial Council reports that **over 50% of California's 58 courts cannot routinely cover non-mandated cases** (civil, family, probate), and **over 30% can never provide CSRs in those cases**.

That gives us:
- **~30+ counties** where reporters are "rarely" available for our target case types
- **~18 counties** where reporters are "never" available

The 10 counties listed above are the publicly-documented subset. There are an estimated 8 more "never" counties that have quietly stopped without issuing splashy press releases. For TAM modeling, treat the list above as a **confirmed floor**, not a complete enumeration.

### Counties still providing reporters in family law (per 2023 Law360)

**Orange, Riverside,** and (at the time) San Bernardino. San Bernardino has since pulled civil/probate but kept family. **Orange and Riverside status should be re-verified before pitching firms there**. If their courts are still covering family law internally, our pitch in those counties has to lean on workflow / billing / paneling value rather than the access-to-justice framing.

San Francisco status is unconfirmed and needs to be verified before we add it to any tier.

## Three-tier county classification

This is the operational tiering that drives our launch sequence and our outreach prioritization. Tiers reflect (a) whether courts have stopped supplying reporters, and (b) whether ER has been authorized beyond the statutory minimum.

### Tier A – Live + audio active demand (launch here first)

Both no-reporter and ER-expansion in place. We can sell both SKUs (live-attendance booking and post-recording audio transcription) without political friction.

- **Los Angeles**
- **Santa Clara**
- **Contra Costa**
- **Alameda** (pending citation; Rosie added 2026-05-18)

### Tier B – Live demand, statutory ER still blocked

Reporters not provided in our target case types, but ER is still capped at § 69957's minor-matters scope. We can sell live-attendance booking here, but the audio-transcription SKU is limited to what the statute already permits. Expansion candidates if the FVAP lawsuit or future legislation broadens ER.

- **San Diego**
- **Sacramento**
- **San Bernardino**
- **Santa Barbara**
- **Kings**
- **Solano**
- **Fresno**
- **Del Norte**

### Tier C – Reporter still provided, monitor

Courts are still supplying reporters in family / probate / civil. Demand for our marketplace exists but is workflow-driven (billing reconciliation, paneling, transcript-correction handling) rather than access-driven.

- **Orange** (verify)
- **Riverside** (verify)
- **San Francisco** (verify)
- Remaining 40+ counties not classified above (verify case-by-case as we expand)

## Pilot launch recommendation

**Pilot region: Bay Area trio – Santa Clara + Contra Costa + Alameda.**

Why these three together:
- All three are Tier A (ER orders + no-reporter status).
- Geographically tight: one CSR can serve all three, which directly addresses our cold-start supply-liquidity problem.
- Rosie has personal network reach across all three.
- Demand is the most acute political-pressure point in the state – attorneys actively unable to get a record on routine matters.

**Pilot success metric** (proposed, from Claude Chat strategy session):
- 50 confirmed bookings across 20+ distinct law firms in 90 days
- < 48 hours average time-to-CSR-match

If we hit those numbers in the Bay Area, the LA expansion case writes itself.

### Why not LA first

LA is the largest TAM, and we'll need it. But for a v1 launch:
- 75+ family courtrooms across multiple courthouses – operationally heavy
- Fragmented attorney community
- Entrenched depo vendors who will reactively expand into court work the moment they see traction
- Active CSR union hostility to anything that touches ER (LA union has publicly accused LASC of misspending CSR recruitment funds and actively opposes ER)

Don't pick a fight in your first 100 bookings. Build evidence in the Bay Area, then expand to LA from a position of demonstrated demand.

### Why not San Diego first

Demand has been pent up since Nov 2021 and is tempting. But San Diego has no ER order, so we'd be selling only the live half of the product in a market where supply scarcity is worst. We'd be promising what we can't reliably deliver.

### Audio-transcription SKU constraints

The audio-transcription SKU (post-hearing recording → certified transcript) is only viable where ER is authorized by court order or statute. So:
- **Tier A counties:** audio-transcription SKU is fully on the table for our priority case types (family, probate, civil)
- **Tier B counties:** audio-transcription SKU only applies to the limited-civil / misdemeanor / infraction matters where § 69957 already allows ER. Family / probate / unlimited civil audio jobs in Tier B counties would require a one-off court order.
- **Tier C counties:** ER is still rare; audio SKU is opportunistic at best.

This has implications for which CSRs we recruit – see the marketing-plan updates for the four-cohort priority (especially cohort 4: out-of-state CSRs and qualified transcriptionists for the audio SKU).

## Strategic rationale for going court-first

Three reasons (from Claude Chat, paraphrased for our context):

1. **The wedge is hotter without depos.** The court-record crisis is an active access-to-justice story with no incumbent solving it well. The depo market is mature and dominated by Veritext, US Legal Support, Esquire – we'd be entering as a fourth-best option. Court-first gives us a story that depo-first can't.

2. **The audio-transcription SKU is uniquely ours.** No existing depo vendor is set up for the post-ER audio-transcription workflow that LA and Santa Clara just created. That's a first-mover lane that disappears if we wait.

3. **Supply liquidity drives our cold-start.** The CSR cohorts we can recruit fastest (retired / semi-retired CSRs, newly-licensed CSRs without agency relationships) are people who'll happily take court work but aren't competing for depo bookings. Court-first matches the supply we can actually capture.

**The risk we're managing is not "launching too narrow" – it's "being seen as a feature, not a platform."** As long as the product story makes the depo path obvious as a future expansion (without making depo support a v1 commitment), court-only at launch is the right call.

**Note on depos in roadmap:** Claude Chat suggested making depos a public 6–12 month roadmap item. This **conflicts** with `governance-and-process/scope_public_court_proceedings_only.md` and the `project_no_concierge_depos.md` memory, which currently say we won't act as a depo booking agent because scheduling complexity is the blocker, not legal risk. Resolution needed before we make any public statements about depo roadmap. Flagged for Aud + Rosie discussion.

## CSR cohorts to capture (priority order)

This is the supply-side breakdown that pairs with the demand-side tiering above. It's reflected in detail in `marketing_plan_phase1_csr_supply.md`; summarized here for one-stop strategic context.

1. **Recently retired or semi-retired CSRs** – the gold mine. 70%+ of LASC's 315 reporters are eligible to retire today, and similar age profiles exist statewide. They don't want a full courthouse job but will take a 2-hour family law hearing on their schedule. Reach via CCRA alumni lists, retirement-transition forums, Rosie's network.
2. **Freelance CSRs currently doing depos who hate the agency cut.** Veritext / US Legal Support take 30–50% off the top. Our offer: $0 platform cut for CSRs ($99/yr listing only), 12% attorney-side fee, CSR keeps 100% of stated rate. They already have the workflow, equipment, and pro tem experience.
3. **Newly-licensed CSRs locked out of established networks.** Only 39 licenses were issued in 2021, down from 96 in 2015; 8 training programs left in California. Partner directly with the remaining 8 programs to capture grads. Long-term supply pipeline.
4. **Out-of-state CSRs + qualified transcriptionists for the audio-transcription SKU.** Once an ER recording exists, the transcription work doesn't require a CA-licensed CSR being physically present – it requires someone qualified to produce a certified transcript from audio. Dramatically wider labor pool than the live-only model can access.

### What NOT to do in CSR recruiting

Do not pitch this as anti-CSR or pro-ER. The CSR union is actively hostile to ER expansion. Position Shorthanders as pro-CSR-income: we're helping reporters monetize the same work better, including new revenue from transcribing court audio that's currently going to nobody. Open-marketplace framing (CSRs set their own rates, keep 100%) reinforces this.
