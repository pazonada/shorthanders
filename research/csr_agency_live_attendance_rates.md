# California CSR Live-Attendance Rate Survey

**Purpose:** Inform Dewey Sholes pricing for live court-attendance (per-diem / appearance-fee) bookings. Focus is **court hearings**, not depositions. Depositions are a separate, generally higher-priced private market and are flagged but excluded from the hearing-only averages.

**Observation date:** 2026-05-12. All amounts are USD.

**Methodology:** Web searches across CSR agency websites, California Superior Court fee schedules, the Judicial Council's legislative report on Gov. Code § 68086 collections, Cal-CCRA materials, and industry pricing guides. Many agency sites are quote-walled, so the agency sample is necessarily thin. Where a number is not published, the entry is marked **N/P** (not published).

**Important distinction:**
- **Court per-diem (under Gov. Code § 68086)** is what the court itself charges parties when an *official* reporter is provided. These amounts are statute-driven and equal "actual cost" (salary + benefits + up to 18% indirect cost, per Cal. Rules of Court 2.958). They are benchmarks, not market prices.
- **Private agency rates** for *pro tem* / contract reporters at court hearings sit above the official-reporter cost-recovery rate. Where they were findable, they cluster meaningfully higher than the court-set numbers.

### Why WC reporters earn this much: the O+2 structural quirk

Tina Sherman explained the structural reason CA WC pays so well: "In California it's an automatic O+2." That is, **every** workers-comp deposition transcript is automatically produced as Original + 2 certified copies, regardless of how many parties separately order. The per-page rate applies to each deliverable.

| Deliverable | Pages | Rate (illustrative) | Subtotal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original | 100 | $3.00–5.00/pg | $300–500 |
| Copy 1 (certified) | 100 | $1.75/pg | $175 |
| Copy 2 (certified) | 100 | $1.75/pg | $175 |
| **Total per 100-page WC depo** | | | **$650–850** |

A non-WC civil deposition typically delivers Original + 1 simultaneous copy (or original only) — roughly half the gross per identical job. WC's automatic O+2 effectively doubles the transcript billing baseline.

**Structural reason (statutory):** CA WC cases have at least three required-document recipients — applicant, defense/carrier, sometimes WCAB or defense expert. Lab. Code § 5710 and 8 CCR § 10579 drive the deliverable expectation. Industry practice is to bill all three upfront rather than chase per-party orders.

**Implication for DS:**

1. **Multi-copy billing must be modeled cleanly.** Original rate ≠ copy rate. The default deliverable structure varies by case type (WC = auto O+2; civil non-WC = O+1 or O-only). The current DS pricing model has [simultaneousCopy: 0.65, separateCopy: 0.85] in `index.html:2747` but no mechanism to default a job to O+2 vs. O+1 vs. O-only based on case type.

2. **WCAB is a real revenue opportunity, not a fringe.** Rosie's 5/12 answer confirmed WCAB in scope. Tina's $200–264K/year income validates that the niche is meaningful, not anecdotal. Tina is the prototype DS supply target: remote, niche-focused, runs her own workflow, curates her hours.

3. **The "CSR shortage" pitch needs precision.** The shortage is acute for *in-person court attendance in under-served counties* (Contra Costa, San Diego). For *remote depo work in focused niches*, the CSRs who've adapted are thriving. Marketing should be specific about which CSR segment we're solving for.

4. **Tina is a credibility-grade voice in the FB group** (14 reactions, anonymous "this is incredible!!" reply). If Rosie's network has any path to her, she's a clear early-supply target — similar to Caldarella. Worth flagging for outreach.

## 3. Summary statistics

### 3a. Agency sample – hearing rates
Only **1 of 17** surveyed agencies published a court-hearing per-diem rate (Denise Paddock CSR, OC-based solo agency / approved on LA, Riverside, OC lists):
- Full-day court appearance: **$1,900 base**, with a $750 deposition minimum for comparison.
- This data point is South-of-Bay-Area; no Bay Area agency publishes a hearing rate publicly.

Industry-aggregate references (not single-firm, but published):
- **Judicial Council estimate:** ≈ **$3,300/day** average for a privately-hired court reporter (per Bay Area Legal Aid FAQ citing Judicial Council).
- **StenoScout 2026 guide:** sessions span **$250–$1,500+**, realtime +10–50%, expedited transcript +50–100%.

Because the agency sample with published hearing rates is n = 1, **mean / median / range on the private side cannot be honestly computed**. The honest range from the available signals is roughly **$1,500–$3,300 per full day for court hearings**, with the lower end being for a basic motion appearance and the upper end being a trial day with realtime/expedited transcript add-ons.

### 3b. Court statutory per-diems (§ 68086, party-paid)
Counting LA, SF, Alameda (2015), San Bernardino, Riverside — n = 5 published amounts.
- **Half-day:** range **$280 – $440**; mean **$348.80**; median **$326**.
- **Full-day:** range **$560 – $880**; mean **$691.20**; median **$631**.

### 3c. Bay Area only (LA stripped out)
Court statutory per-diems – SF + Alameda (2015 figure) = n = 2:
- Half-day: **$280–$316**; mean **$298**; median **$298**.
- Full-day: **$560–$631**; mean **$595.50**; median **$595.50**.

Court employer-side per-diem pay-rate to the reporter – Contra Costa + San Mateo (Bay Area only) = n = 2:
- Half-day: **$250–$500**; mean **$375**; median **$375**.
- Full-day: **$500–$1,000**; mean **$750**; median **$750**.

No Bay Area private agency published a court-hearing rate, so a Bay Area private average is **not computable from public data**.

### 3d. Deposition reference (separate market, excluded from above)
Published industry references put deposition appearance fees at roughly **$400–$700 baseline**, with Denise Paddock CSR's published deposition minimum at **$750** plus $100–$300/hr. These rates are systematically higher than court-hearing rates from the same reporter because depositions are private, scheduled-by-the-attorney work and almost always include a transcript order with a per-page premium.

### The clear CA-vs-FL premium

FL data points cluster **$340-$1,000** (low end private CSRs; top end is the state-contract Lexitas rate).

CA data points cluster **$1,900-$3,600** (Denise Paddock published rate as the floor; Rosie's Contra Costa and the JC aggregate as the higher end).

**CA is roughly 2-3× FL** for the same work. Several plausible reasons:
- Cost of living premium (especially Bay Area / LA)
- CA's well-documented reporter shortage drives up private-market rates
- CA's statutory page rate is a floor that doesn't constrain appearance fees, leaving more room for private market premium
- FL's heavy use of digital court reporting suppresses private-CSR demand and pricing

### What this means for DS recalibration

The "Bay Area court statutory per-diem" anchors I previously leaned on ($316 half / $631 full SF) are **cost-recovery floors for court-employed reporters**, not market prices. Private CA reporters bill substantially above that.

The right pricing anchors for the Bay Area private market are the CA-specific data points: **$1,800-3,600 full-day**. To position DS as meaningfully cheaper than agencies (per Rosie's "win-win" vision) while still paying CSRs well above agency take-out:

| Tier | Half-day | Full-day | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor — typical CA private agency | $1,800 | ~$2,500-3,600 | What Bay Area attorneys actually pay agencies today |
| DS pilot — ~50% below agency | $900 | $1,500 | Significant attorney savings; CSR keeps 100% (vs. 30-60% via agency); above FL Lexitas state contract |
| **DS pilot — split the difference** (recommended) | **$1,050** | **$1,650** | The "I don't want to undersell, but I don't want to overshoot before data" hedge — between the $900/$1,500 conservative and $1,200/$1,800 aggressive |
| DS pilot — more aggressive (~35% below agency) | $1,200 | $1,800 | Closer to private agency floor; strong CSR raise |
| DS pilot — conservative (~70% below agency) | $700 | $1,200 | Still well above court statutory ($596 Bay Area mean); safer if CSRs balk |
| Floor I wouldn't go below | $500 | $900 | Anything lower undersells the work; CSRs do this rate through agencies today |

Note: this revises the earlier "$600 half / $1,100 full" guess upward. The earlier number was anchored too heavily on FL rate cards and Bay Area court statutory; with CA-specific evidence properly weighted, the recommended landing zone is **$1,050 / $1,650** — between the two original tiers. The ±20% CSR rate-setting band means premium CSRs can position at $1,260/$1,980 (top of band) and cold-start CSRs can sit at $840/$1,320 (bottom of band), giving the platform a published *middle* rate rather than a ceiling or floor.

**Survey is the validator.** Final landing should be confirmed by Stage 1 CSR survey results (Q7/Q8 ask CSRs their own private-direct rates). If 30-60 CSR responses cluster materially above or below $1,650 full-day, adjust before pilot launch.

The ±20% CSR rate-setting band (per AB-5 prong 3) means individual CSRs can adjust their published rate up to $1,080/$1,800 (top of band) or down to $720/$1,200 (bottom of band). Premium CSRs (Denise Paddock-tier) can position at the top; cold-start CSRs can use the bottom of the band for visibility.

### Trial-day implications

Current code: $2,200/trial-day. Most private cards add a *trial premium* on top of standard full-day rather than doubling it:
- Card 6 example: ~$910 weekday in-person regular + $400 jury-trial premium ≈ $1,310 jury trial day
- Card 1 FL: $240 base + $240 in-person + $200 jury trial premium = $680 jury trial day
- Card 7 FL: $570 weekday trial appearance (lower-tier rate card)

If DS full-day lands at $1,500, **trial-day at 1.3-1.5× full-day = $1,950-$2,250**. Current $2,200 is roughly right under that math, but should be reframed in code as "full-day × trial premium" rather than a standalone number — so the trial rate updates when the full-day rate moves.

## 5. Honest gaps

- **15 of 17 surveyed agencies are quote-walled** – they show service descriptions and contact forms only, no rate sheets. This is the dominant practice in the CA court-reporting industry; firms guard pricing as competitive information and tailor quotes per attorney/firm.
- **Only 1 agency (Denise Paddock CSR)** published a court-appearance full-day per-diem on its public site. That data point is also a solo-reporter agency in Orange County, so it represents one reporter's pricing rather than agency-wide multi-reporter pricing.
- **No Bay Area private agency** (Aiken & Welch, Behmke, Combs, Jan Brown, Veritext-SF, Esquire-SF, etc.) publishes a court-hearing rate publicly. To get true Bay Area private comps for pricing the marketplace, the realistic paths are (a) RFQ-style outreach as a fake attorney persona, (b) Rosie's existing relationships with CSRs to share what they actually charge, or (c) interviews with attorney users about their last-paid rates.
- **Court fee schedules vary by year.** The Alameda numbers in circulation are from 2015 ($280 / $560); the 2021 emergency schedule says "set by court." The current 2025 Alameda figure is not crisply published in a single PDF – the search index returns the older schedule. SF's $316 / $631 is from the 2025 Statewide Civil Fee Schedule and is current.
- **Santa Clara does not publish a half-day / full-day § 68086 fee** in the search index – only the $30 minimum-fee line for short hearings. The local fee page directs filers to the most recent PDF, which sets the amount but is not surfaced in raw search results.
- **OAH contract rates** are real but live inside a "Price Book" document that the DGS contract page links to rather than displaying. The annual rate is current to the 2025-04-01 to 2026-03-31 cycle.
- **Distinction between hearing and deposition pricing was conflated** in many secondary sources (legal-blog summaries). I have tried to keep them separate, but if a published rate did not clearly state "court appearance" it was excluded from the hearing-only count.
- **Multi-day trial rates were not separately published** by any surveyed agency. The standard practice (per Denise Paddock's site) is that full-day per-diem applies, with possible expedited-transcript premiums.
- **No agency published a discount tier for repeat or marketplace bookings**, which is the relevant pricing comp for Dewey Sholes' 12% buyer-fee model.
