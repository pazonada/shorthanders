# Law student ambassador program

**Owner:** Aud
**Last updated:** 2026-05-02
**Status:** Concept — not yet activated

---

## The idea

We need physical presence at events where legal assistants and paralegals gather (bar association meetings, paralegal CE seminars, professional association events). But we can't afford to fly Aud or Rosie to every event, and we definitely can't afford full-cost sponsorships at most of them.

**Hire law students as paid event ambassadors.** They want to attend these events anyway for networking. We pay them an hourly rate to:
- Wear branded Dewey Sholes gear (jacket, hat, button/pin, polo)
- Hand out branded swag (stickers, business cards, postcards with QR codes)
- Make conversation with attendees, especially legal assistants and paralegals
- Collect business cards or contact info from interested attendees
- Take photos of the event for our social media (with consent)
- Report back on who they met and what conversations stuck

This is cheaper than sponsorship, more scalable than founder attendance, and gives the platform a human face at events we couldn't otherwise be at.

---

## Why law students specifically

- **They want to be there anyway.** Networking at legal industry events is part of how they build their early careers. Getting paid to do it is a bonus.
- **They look the part.** A 25-year-old in a polo with a Dewey Sholes logo is more credible than a 22-year-old marketing intern who's never been in a courtroom.
- **They speak the language.** They've taken civ pro, they know what a deposition is, they understand what a transcript is. Conversation with attendees flows naturally.
- **They're cheap.** $20-30/hour for event work is well above minimum wage for them, well below what a full-time hire costs us.
- **They're motivated.** Most are looking for any excuse to network. They'll genuinely engage.
- **Bay Area + LA + SD have many law schools** within reach of major event venues.

## Hiring & paying ambassadors

### Profile we want
- Currently enrolled at a CA law school (UC Hastings, Berkeley, USF, Stanford, USC, UCLA, Loyola, Pepperdine, USD, Hastings, etc.)
- 2L or 3L preferred (more confidence, more legal vocabulary)
- Comfortable approaching strangers and making small talk
- Interested in litigation track (they'll get the value prop instinctively)
- Has reliable transportation to event venues

### Compensation
- **$25/hour** for event hours (4-8 hour shifts typical)
- **Travel reimbursement** for events more than 30 miles from their school
- **Gear stipend** — branded polo, jacket, hat, lanyard provided free; they keep what fits and looks professional
- **Bonus structure (optional)**: $5 per qualified lead (legal assistant or paralegal whose info they captured), capped at $50/event

**Estimated cost per event:** $100-200 for a typical 4-6 hour event, plus travel.

### Where to find them
- Law school career services offices — post a paid gig listing
- Law school student government / professional organizations (ABA Law Student Division chapters, paralegal student associations)
- LinkedIn (search for current law students in the city)
- Referrals from first hires (they'll know other students looking for similar work)

## How we equip them

### Branded gear (provided by us, kept by them)
- Polo or quarter-zip with Dewey Sholes logo (~$25 each from Custom Ink or similar)
- Optional: branded baseball cap (~$15)
- Pin/button to attach to existing professional clothing for less branded events (~$2)

For a small upfront investment ($40-60 per ambassador), we get a walking advertisement at every event they attend.

### Swag they hand out
- Branded business cards with QR code linking to landing page (~$30 for 500 cards)
- Stickers (~$50 for 100 quality vinyl stickers)
- Branded postcards with the value prop on one side and QR code on the other (~$80 for 250 postcards)
- Optional: small pen-and-notepad sets (~$2-3 each)
- Optional: stress balls or other tchotchkes (~$1-2 each)

**Recommended minimum kit:** business cards + stickers + postcards = ~$160 covers ~10 events.

### Conversation script
We give them a 1-page cheat sheet:
- 30-second elevator pitch
- 3 sample opening lines for approaching people
- The 3 main pain points we solve (chasing reporters, opaque pricing, slow payments)
- The 3 features that close it (cost-split, ratings, deadline-driven pricing)
- The pilot offer ($25 gift card after 3 jobs, free CSR listing for first year)
- A "talk to my founder" handoff line for when someone wants more info

## Event types we target

### Tier 1: Cheap and high-value (always go)
- **Bar association support staff committee meetings** (free or member-only)
- **Paralegal association monthly meetups** ($10-25 entry, often dinner included)
- **Law school career fairs** (free to attend, sometimes free swag table)
- **Continuing education seminars for paralegals** ($20-100 entry, but high concentration of target users)

### Tier 2: Mid-cost, evaluate per event
- **Statewide paralegal conferences** (LAPA, SFPA, OCPA — $100-300 entry)
- **Regional bar association annual meetings** (variable cost; sometimes have unattended exhibit tables for $200-500)
- **CLE seminars on litigation topics** (more attorneys than legal assistants, but useful for cross-promotion)

### Tier 3: Skip for now
- **Large state-wide attorney conferences** (CA State Bar Annual Meeting — $5K+ for booth, mostly attorneys not legal assistants)
- **National conferences** (NALA, NFPA — too far, too expensive, too broad)

## Cost per qualifying lead

### Cheap event scenario
- Free entry, ambassador attends for 3 hours, hands out 30 cards
- Cost: $75 (ambassador) + $20 (printed materials used) = $95
- If 5 qualified leads → $19/lead. Excellent.

### Mid-cost event scenario
- $200 unattended exhibit table, ambassador attends for 6 hours
- Cost: $200 (table) + $150 (ambassador) + $30 (materials) = $380
- If 15 qualified leads → $25/lead. Solid.

### Comparison: paid ads
- LinkedIn ads to legal assistants: typical CPL is $50-150
- Google search ads for "court reporter California": $5-15/click but most clicks don't convert

**Conclusion:** Event-based outreach via law student ambassadors should be a more cost-effective channel than paid ads for our specific target audience.

## Risks

- **Bad ambassador experience.** Untrained or disinterested ambassador hurts the brand more than no ambassador. Vet carefully, have a written brief, debrief after every event.
- **Wasted events.** Some events may not have enough legal assistants to justify the trip. Filter aggressively.
- **Compliance / professional courtesy.** Some bar association meetings may not allow vendor solicitation. Always confirm with the event organizer first. Sometimes we sponsor as a "supporter" rather than actively pitching.
- **CA labor classification.** $25/hour W-2 contractor work is fine for occasional event gigs, but if we end up paying any one ambassador for many hours/week, we should treat them as a part-time employee with proper paperwork.
