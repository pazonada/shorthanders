# Marketing plan: Phase 2 — Law firm demand acquisition

**Owner:** Aud (execution) + Rosie (warm intros + closer)
**Last updated:** 2026-04-11
**Status:** Draft
**Budget:** ~$0 (sweat equity only)
**Timeline:** Month 3-5 (after 100 CSRs signed up)
**Goal:** 15-25 firms actively using the platform

---

## Prerequisites

- [ ] 100+ CSRs signed up on platform (Phase 1 complete)
- [ ] Site build complete and functional (order flow, payment, delivery)
- [ ] At least a few successful test transactions completed

## Pilot pricing for firms

- **Platform fee: 8%** (vs standard 12%) for firms that sign up during pilot
- 8% rate extends through the **first full year**
- After Year 1: standard 12% rate applies
- Pilot users get personal onboarding support and direct access to the team during the 6-month pilot window

## Target firm profile

| Attribute | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Size | 2-20 attorneys |
| Practice area | PI, family law, employment, insurance defense, civil lit |
| Location | Any CA metro (transcription is statewide) |
| Current CSR sourcing | Word of mouth, outdated agency, or struggling to find reporters |
| Decision-maker | Paralegal, legal secretary, or office manager (not the partner) |
| Monthly transcript volume | 3+ orders/month |

### Critical insight: target the paralegal, not the partner

Per Rosie: the senior attorney doesn't care HOW the firm gets the transcript — they just want it delivered on time. The paralegal or legal secretary is the one who actually finds and books reporters. They are the real user and the real decision-maker for platform adoption.

## Channels (all free)

### 1. Rosie's network (Primary — highest conversion)
**Source:** 22 years of family law practice in CA
**Approach:**
- Personal outreach to firms Rosie has worked with
- Focus on the paralegal/secretary at each firm, not just the attorney
- Ask: "Who handles your transcript ordering? Can I show them something that'll save them time?"
- Demo the platform in a 15-minute screen share

**Target:** 5-10 firms
**Timeline:** Month 3, ongoing

### 2. Reporter-to-firm intros (supply-side leverage)
**Source:** CSRs who signed up in Phase 1
**Approach:**
- Ask signed-up reporters: "Which firms do you work with regularly?"
- Reporter sends a warm intro: "Hey, I'm on this new platform — you can book me through it"
- The firm tries the platform because their trusted reporter is already there
- This is the most powerful channel because it's trust-based on both sides

**Target:** 5-10 firms
**Timeline:** Month 3-4, as reporter relationships develop

### 3. Cold email to litigation firms
**Source:** Firm targeting list (from CA_Pilot_Firm_Targeting.md)
**Approach:**
- Target paralegals and legal secretaries (find names from firm websites, LinkedIn)
- Email sequence:
  1. Pain point: "Tired of chasing court reporters? Book a licensed, verified CSR in minutes."
  2. Social proof: "X reporters already on the platform in [their metro area]"
  3. Offer: "8% platform fee for the first year during our pilot — half our standard rate"
- Personalize by practice area and location

**Target:** 5-10 firms (from a larger outreach of 200-500 contacts)
**Timeline:** Month 3-4

### 4. LinkedIn content (demand-side messaging)
**Source:** Aud's marketing/comms background
**Approach:**
- Shift content focus from CSR shortage (Phase 1) to attorney pain points:
  - "How much does your firm spend on transcript logistics?"
  - "The hidden cost of chasing court reporters"
  - "Statutory rates vs. agency markups — what you're actually paying"
- Tag/mention paralegals and litigation support professionals
- Share pilot success stories as they happen

**Target:** Brand awareness, 2-5 inbound signups
**Timeline:** Ongoing from Month 3

### 5. Local bar association listservs and newsletters
**Source:** Family law sections, litigation sections, paralegal associations
**Approach:**
- Rosie posts to family law bar association listservs (she's a member)
- Submit announcements to local bar newsletters
- Many allow member announcements for free
- Key metros: LA County Bar, SF Bar, San Diego County Bar, OC Bar

**Target:** 3-5 firms
**Timeline:** Month 3-4

### 6. Paralegal association meetups and events
**Source:** SF Paralegal Association, LA Paralegal Association, Sacramento, San Diego
**Approach:**
- Attend monthly meetings (free or $10-20 entry)
- Bring business cards, give a 2-minute pitch if there's an open floor
- Paralegals talk to each other — one enthusiastic user can spread the word across firms
- Offer to do a short presentation on "how to find court reporters faster" (educational, not salesy)

**Target:** Relationship building, 2-5 firms
**Timeline:** Month 4-5

## Onboarding flow for firms

Once a firm signs up, the first experience matters:

1. **First order:** Aud or Rosie personally monitors it. Make sure a reporter accepts quickly and delivery is smooth.
2. **Follow-up call:** 24 hours after first delivery. "How was it? Anything we can improve?"
3. **Second order nudge:** If no second order within 2 weeks, check in. "Need help with anything?"
4. **Referral ask:** After 3 successful orders: "Know another firm that would benefit?"

The pilot is small enough that every firm gets white-glove treatment.

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Firms sign up but don't order | White-glove onboarding, personal follow-up, make first order frictionless |
| No reporter available for a job | Ensure 100+ CSR supply before starting; manually match early jobs if needed |
| Paralegals resistant to change | Focus on firms already frustrated with current process; let early adopters evangelize |
| Firms want features we don't have yet | Document requests, prioritize for build, be transparent about roadmap |
