# Briscoe Sholes — Attorney survey: staff time costs on transcript ordering
*Draft — March 13, 2026*

---

## Purpose

This survey quantifies how much time and money law firms currently spend on the transcript ordering process. The data feeds directly into two things: (1) the platform fee financial model (what is the firm's willingness to pay for time savings?) and (2) marketing claims ("firms save X hours per month on transcript admin").

Rosie distributes this to her network. Target: 15-25 responses from CA litigation firms.

---

## Survey design notes

- Keep it under 5 minutes. Attorneys won't complete anything longer.
- Mix of multiple choice and short numeric answers. No open-ended essays.
- Anonymous by default, with an optional field to share firm name for follow-up.
- Can be built in Google Forms, Typeform, or similar.

## Analysis framework

Once responses are collected, compute:

1. **Weighted average staff time per order** — use Q7 midpoints weighted by response count
2. **Hourly cost of that time** — use standard paralegal billing rates ($75-150/hr) and attorney rates ($200-500/hr) based on Q5 (who does the work)
3. **Monthly cost per firm** — staff time per order × orders per month (Q3) × hourly rate
4. **Pain point priority ranking** — aggregate Q11 rankings
5. **Fee tolerance distribution** — Q13 histogram
6. **Beta interest pipeline** — Q14 + Q15 for follow-up

Key metrics for the financial model:
- If the median firm orders 5 transcripts/month and spends 1.5 hours each at $100/hr paralegal rate, that's $750/month in admin costs. A 15% platform fee on a $170 transcript is ~$25. The value proposition: pay $125/month in platform fees to save $750 in staff time.
