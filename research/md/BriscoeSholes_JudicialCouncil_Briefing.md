*Draft — March 13, 2026*

## Who we are

Briscoe Sholes is a technology platform that connects California law firms with licensed court reporters for transcript ordering, payment, and delivery. One co-founder is a California attorney with 22 years of litigation practice. The other has a technology background. The platform is in beta development with planned launch in select California counties.

Our website is briscoesholes.com. Direct inquiries to hello@briscoesholes.com.

## The problem we address

The Judicial Council's own data tells the story. Between September 2023 and July 2024, over 1 million proceedings took place without a verbatim record. The Feb 2025 Supreme Court order named specific counties and demanded action plans. New CSR license applications have dropped 38%, and roughly half the current reporter workforce is eligible to retire.

The reporter shortage is real and will take years to reverse through recruitment and training pipelines. In the meantime, every day produces more proceedings without transcripts and more litigants without access to their own records.

Briscoe Sholes does not solve the supply problem directly. No platform can manufacture court reporters. But we can make better use of the reporters who exist — by reducing the friction in how firms find them, order from them, pay them, and receive transcripts. We can also bring semi-retired CSRs back into the workflow by offering flexible, remote transcription work that fits their schedules.

---

## How the platform works

The platform operates in two modes, designed to fit the two county models that exist in California today.

**Staff-reporter counties.** Most counties still assign live reporters to proceedings. After the hearing, the firm contacts the reporter directly to order a transcript. This post-hearing process is where the friction lives: back-and-forth on turnaround time, payment arrangements, W-9 collection, delivery follow-up. Briscoe Sholes replaces that process with a single transaction. The reporter is the same person from the courtroom. The platform handles ordering, payment (within 48 hours of delivery), tax compliance, and status tracking.

**ER counties.** In counties with expanded electronic recording — LA, Riverside, San Bernardino, Santa Clara, and others — there is no reporter at the hearing. If a party wants a transcript, they need to find a licensed reporter willing to transcribe from audio. This is the most acute pain point. The platform matches firms with available reporters based on location, practice area, availability, and turnaround time. Pricing follows statutory rates. Expedite fees are set by each reporter and disclosed to the firm before the order is placed.

## Alignment with Judicial Council priorities

We've read the Council's public materials on the court reporter shortage, the expanded ER program, and the access-to-justice implications of the transcript gap. The platform aligns with several stated priorities.

**Transcript access for fee-waiver litigants.** The platform supports TRF-eligible orders as a workflow feature. The firm or SRL pays the reporter through the platform at the statutory rate, and the platform generates the TRF reimbursement application with all required fields pre-filled. The firm or SRL submits the application to the CRB for reimbursement. The platform simplifies the ordering and paperwork process; it does not front the reimbursement. Improving transcript access for litigants who cannot afford to pay is built into the platform from day one.

**Workforce utilization.** The reporter shortage is partly a distribution problem. Reporters in one county may have capacity while firms in a neighboring county can't find anyone. The platform creates visibility across geographic lines. It also creates a pathway for semi-retired CSRs — an estimated half of the current workforce — to continue taking work on a flexible, remote basis.

**ER county support.** The expanded electronic recording program solves the in-courtroom problem but creates a downstream transcript problem. Someone still has to transcribe the audio. The platform provides the marketplace that ER counties need to connect audio recordings with available reporters.

**Statutory rate integrity.** The platform enforces Gov. Code section 69950 rates programmatically. Per-word rates cannot be overridden. Expedite fees are disclosed upfront. The platform fee is charged to the ordering firm as a separate, transparent line item and does not reduce the reporter's statutory compensation.

## What the platform does not do

It may be helpful to clarify what Briscoe Sholes is not.

It is not a court reporting agency. We do not employ reporters, assign them to hearings, or supervise their work. Reporters are independent contractors who choose which jobs to accept and deliver certified transcripts under their own CSR license.

It is not a replacement for court-employed reporters. In staff-reporter counties, the platform sits downstream of the hearing. The reporter was already there. The platform streamlines what happens after.

It is not an alternative to the CRB's licensing requirements. Every reporter on the platform must hold an active California CSR license. We verify against the CRB's public database and can implement periodic re-verification.

It does not edit, review, or certify transcripts. The reporter produces the transcript. The platform moves it from point A to point B and handles the financial transaction.

## What we'd like from the Judicial Council

We are not asking for an endorsement or formal partnership at this stage. We are requesting the following.

**A meeting with relevant staff.** We'd like to present the platform to staff in the Court Reporters Program, the Language Access & Court Reporters Unit, or another appropriate division. We want to understand any concerns the Council might have and incorporate feedback before launch.

**Data sharing, if appropriate.** The Council tracks ER order data, reporter vacancy rates, and TRF utilization by county. If any of this data is available to outside parties, it would help us target our launch to counties where the need is greatest.

**Visibility into policy changes.** Pending legislation (and the Supreme Court's ongoing engagement) may change the landscape for ER counties, reporter licensing, or transcript pricing. We want to stay informed so the platform remains compliant with whatever the Council and Legislature decide.

**Consideration as a pilot partner.** If the Council is exploring technology solutions to the transcript access problem — whether through the ER program, the TRF, or other channels — we'd welcome the opportunity to be considered as a pilot partner. We can provide data on transcript turnaround times, reporter utilization, and firm ordering patterns that the Council may find useful for policy purposes.

## Questions we anticipate

**Does this compete with the court's own reporter programs?**
No. In staff-reporter counties, we supplement the existing system by handling the post-hearing ordering workflow. In ER counties, we fill a gap that currently has no systematic solution. The platform works alongside court programs, not against them.

**How do you handle TRF orders?**
The platform supports TRF-eligible orders as a workflow feature. The firm or SRL pays the reporter through the platform at the statutory rate — the same as any other order — and the reporter receives payment within 48 hours of delivery. The platform then generates the TRF reimbursement application with all required fields pre-filled, which the firm or SRL submits to the CRB. The platform does not front the reimbursement or carry float. The goal is to make the ordering and paperwork as easy as possible while keeping the reimbursement process with the party who has the fee waiver.

**What data would you share with the Council?**
We can provide aggregate, anonymized data on transcript ordering patterns, turnaround times, geographic demand, and reporter utilization. We would not share individual firm or reporter data without consent. We're open to discussing a formal data-sharing arrangement that serves the Council's policy needs.

**What if the statutory rate structure changes?**
The platform's pricing engine is configurable by county and case type. If the Legislature or the Council modifies statutory rates, folio multipliers, or fee structures, we can update the platform to reflect those changes. Compliance is a core design principle, not a bolt-on.
