# Briscoe Sholes — CSR discovery interview guide
*Draft — March 13, 2026*

---

## Purpose

This guide is for conversations with court reporters during the supply-side seeding phase. The goal is to understand their current workflow, pain points, and what would make them willing to use a marketplace platform. These are not sales calls. They are discovery interviews.

Each interview should take 20-30 minutes. Rosie or another team member conducts them by phone or video.

---

## Before the interview

- Confirm the reporter's CSR license is active (check CRB database)
- Note whether they are court-employed, freelance, or both
- Note their geographic coverage if known
- Have the Reporter FAQ ready in case they ask specifics about the platform

---

## Interview structure

### Opening (2 minutes)

"Thanks for making the time. We're building a platform to connect attorneys with court reporters for transcript ordering, and we want to make sure we build it in a way that actually works for reporters. This isn't a sales pitch. We just want to understand how your work currently operates and what's frustrating about it. Everything you share stays confidential."

### Section 1: Current practice (8-10 minutes)

**Q1.** Can you walk me through what a typical week looks like for you? How many hearings or transcription jobs do you handle?

**Q2.** How do you currently get your work? Do attorneys come to you directly, do you work through agencies, are you assigned by the court, or some mix?

**Q3.** What types of proceedings do you cover most often? (family law, civil, criminal, probate, deposition, etc.)

**Q4.** Do you do live attendance only, audio transcription only, or both?

**Q4a.** What percentage of your current work is live court attendance vs. audio transcription? Has that mix changed over the past few years?

**Q5.** How do you set your turnaround time with clients? Is it negotiated per job, or do you have standard timelines?

**Q6.** What does your payment process look like today? How do you invoice, and how long does it typically take to get paid?

### Section 2: Pain points (8-10 minutes)

**Q7.** What's the most frustrating part of the business side of your work? (Not the transcription itself, but everything around it — finding work, getting paid, dealing with attorneys, admin, etc.)

**Q8.** Have you ever done work and had trouble getting paid? How often does that happen?

**Q9.** Do you ever turn down work? If so, why? (Too busy, too far, too low-paying, difficult attorney, etc.)

**Q10.** How do you feel about the current statutory rates? Do they adequately compensate you for your time?

**Q11.** Do you charge expedite fees? If so, how do you determine them? Is it a standard formula or case-by-case?

**Q11a.** For live attendance, what do you typically charge for a half-day hearing? What about a full day? Does that vary by case type or court location? How does that compare to what agencies charge firms for the same work?

**Q12.** Have you dealt with the Transcript Reimbursement Fund? What's been your experience with it?

### Section 3: Technology and platform fit (5-8 minutes)

**Q13.** What technology do you currently use in your work? (CAT software, digital recording tools, case management, invoicing, etc.)

**Q14.** Have you ever used a marketplace or platform to find work? (Anything — not just court reporting. Upwork, freelance sites, agency portals, etc.)

**Q15.** If a platform existed that sent you jobs in your geographic area, handled payment automatically (within 48 hours of delivery), collected W-9s at signup so you never have to deal with that again, and let you set your own availability — would that be interesting to you?

**Q16.** What would concern you about a platform like that? What would you want to know before signing up?

**Q17.** Would you be willing to pay a platform fee (as a percentage of the transcript rate) in exchange for guaranteed payment, a steady flow of jobs, and zero invoicing/collections work? If so, what percentage feels reasonable?

**Q18.** Is there anything about the current system that you'd want to make sure a new platform preserves? Anything that works well that shouldn't be disrupted?

### Section 4: Network and referrals (2-3 minutes)

**Q19.** How connected are you to other reporters in your area? Are there informal networks, group chats, or professional communities where reporters share information?

**Q20.** If you found this platform valuable, would you refer colleagues? What would make you confident enough to do that?

**Q21.** Are you a CCRA member? Do you attend their events?

### Closing (1-2 minutes)

"This has been really helpful. We're going to be launching a beta in [county] in the coming months. Would you be interested in being one of the first reporters on the platform? There's no commitment — we just want early feedback from people who know the work."

- If yes: collect preferred email and phone, note geographic coverage and specialties
- If maybe: ask what additional information would help them decide
- If no: thank them and ask if they'd be willing to refer a colleague who might be interested

## What to listen for

Signals that indicate strong platform fit:
- Frustrated with payment delays or collections
- Already doing freelance work and finding their own clients
- Interested in flexible, remote transcription work (semi-retired segment)
- Comfortable with technology
- Willing to accept a platform fee in exchange for guaranteed payment

Signals that indicate resistance:
- Exclusively court-employed with no interest in freelance work
- Concerned about being "commoditized" or undercut
- Resistant to technology changes
- Worried about being treated as an employee
- Strong loyalty to existing agency relationships

Both types of feedback are valuable. The resistance signals tell you what messaging to avoid and what platform features matter most for adoption.
