# Briscoe Sholes — Court Reporters Board briefing document
*Draft — March 13, 2026*

---

## Who we are

Briscoe Sholes is a technology platform that connects California law firms with licensed court reporters for transcript ordering. One co-founder is a California attorney with 22 years of litigation practice. The other has a technology background. The platform is currently in beta development with a planned launch in select California counties.

Our website is briscoesholes.com. Direct inquiries to hello@briscoesholes.com.

---

## Why we're reaching out to the CRB

The Board's public data shows that new CSR license applications have dropped 38% while roughly half the current workforce is eligible to retire. The Judicial Council has documented over 1 million proceedings without a verbatim record in a ten-month period. These are not numbers anyone involved in court reporting can ignore.

We believe the transcript access problem is partly a supply problem and partly a workflow problem. Licensed reporters exist who could take on more work — particularly semi-retired CSRs who still hold active licenses — but the current system for connecting them with firms that need transcripts is manual, fragmented, and inefficient. Briscoe Sholes is designed to fix the workflow side so the existing supply goes further.

---

## What the platform does

The platform serves two use cases relevant to the Board's mission.

**Ordering layer for staff-reporter counties.** In counties where courts assign their own live reporters, the post-hearing process is still manual. The firm has to contact the reporter, negotiate turnaround, arrange payment, collect a W-9, and follow up on delivery. Briscoe Sholes streamlines this into a single transaction: order, pay, track, receive. The reporter is the same person who was in the courtroom. The platform just removes the back-and-forth.

**Full marketplace for ER counties.** In counties with expanded electronic recording, there is no reporter in the room. If any party wants a transcript, they need to find a reporter willing to transcribe from audio. This is where the shortage is most acute. The platform matches firms with available, licensed reporters based on geographic coverage, practice area, turnaround capability, and availability.

## How we protect CSR interests

We understand the Board exists to protect the public and maintain professional standards. Here is how the platform aligns with that mandate.

**Licensing verification.** Every reporter on the platform must hold an active California CSR license. We verify license status against the CRB's public database at signup and can re-verify periodically. No unlicensed individuals can accept work through the platform.

**Statutory rate compliance.** Per-word transcript rates are set by Government Code section 69950. The platform enforces these rates programmatically. Neither reporters nor firms can override the statutory rate through the platform. The platform fee (charged to the ordering firm) is a separate line item on top of the statutory rate, disclosed upfront in compliance with SB 478.

**Independent contractor status.** Reporters on the platform are independent contractors, not employees. AB 3136 exempts licensed CSRs from the ABC test under AB 5, and our engagement structure is designed to satisfy the Borello test. Reporters choose which jobs to accept, set their own schedules, use their own equipment, and can work through other channels simultaneously.

**Expedite fee transparency.** Expedite fees are within each reporter's discretion. The platform requires reporters to set their expedite fee schedule in advance so firms see the cost before ordering. This benefits both sides: firms get pricing clarity and reporters don't get pressured into unpaid rush work.

**No quality interference.** The platform does not edit, alter, or review transcripts. The reporter produces the certified transcript and delivers it through the platform. Briscoe Sholes is a logistics and payment layer, not a content layer.

## What we'd like from the CRB

We are not asking the Board for an endorsement or a formal partnership at this stage. We would welcome the following.

**A conversation.** We'd like to present the platform to Board staff and hear any concerns about how it intersects with licensing rules, professional standards, or consumer protection mandates. If anything about the platform's design raises red flags, we'd rather know now than after launch.

**Guidance on license verification.** We currently verify CSR status using the Board's public online directory. If there is a preferred method for third-party verification — an API, a bulk data feed, or a formal verification request process — we would like to use it.

**Awareness of our existence.** We expect reporters will contact the Board with questions about the platform. We want the Board to have accurate information so they can respond. We're happy to provide any documentation the Board would find useful.

**Future consideration.** If the Board sees value in a platform that increases transcript access while maintaining professional standards, we'd welcome the opportunity to discuss a more formal relationship down the road — whether that's a listing on the Board's website, inclusion in communications to licensees, or something else entirely.

## Questions we anticipate from the Board

**Does this platform create an employer-employee relationship?**
No. Reporters are independent contractors. They accept or decline jobs at their discretion, use their own tools, and set their own schedules. AB 3136 specifically exempts licensed CSRs from the ABC test.

**Does the platform fee violate statutory rate rules?**
No. The platform fee is charged to the ordering firm on top of the statutory rate, not deducted from the reporter's payment. Reporters receive the full statutory per-word rate for their work. The platform fee covers the matching, payment processing, and compliance services the platform provides to the firm.

**Can unlicensed individuals access the reporter side of the platform?**
No. CSR license verification is required at signup. We can discuss additional safeguards if the Board has suggestions.

**What happens if a reporter's license lapses while they're on the platform?**
We can implement periodic re-verification and automatically suspend a reporter's ability to accept new jobs if their license status changes. We welcome the Board's input on appropriate verification frequency.

**Do you handle fee waiver / Transcript Reimbursement Fund orders?**
Yes. The platform supports TRF-eligible orders as a workflow feature. The firm or SRL pays the reporter through the platform at the statutory rate, and the reporter receives payment within 48 hours of delivery — the same as any other order. The platform then generates the TRF reimbursement application with all required fields pre-filled, which the firm or SRL submits to the CRB. The platform simplifies the ordering and paperwork; it does not front the reimbursement or carry float. Improving transcript access for fee-waiver litigants is a core part of the platform's mission.
