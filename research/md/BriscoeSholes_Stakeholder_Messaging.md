# Briscoe Sholes — Stakeholder messaging framework
*Draft — March 13, 2026*

---

## Purpose

Different stakeholders care about different things. This document provides tailored messaging for each audience Briscoe Sholes will engage during launch and early growth. Each section includes the core message, supporting points, objections to anticipate, and language to avoid.

---

## Law firms (demand side)

### Core message
Order transcripts in minutes, not days. One place to order, pay, track, and receive — with pricing you can see before you commit.

### Supporting points
- Per-word rates are set by statute. Expedite fees vary by reporter and are often unclear. The platform shows you the full cost upfront, before you place the order.
- No more chasing reporters for status updates. Real-time tracking from order to delivery.
- Payment is handled automatically. No more cutting checks, collecting W-9s, or following up on invoices.
- Works for both standard county reporters and ER county transcription orders.
- TRF-eligible orders are supported. The platform generates the reimbursement application paperwork automatically.

### Objections to anticipate
- "We already have reporters we use." — The platform doesn't replace your existing relationships. You can still request specific reporters. It handles the ordering and payment workflow for you regardless of who the reporter is.
- "What's the platform fee?" — It's a percentage on top of the statutory rate, disclosed before you order. Most firms spend 1-2 hours of staff time per transcript order on admin alone. The platform reduces that to minutes.
- "Is this secure?" — We follow SOC 2 principles, encrypt all data in transit and at rest, and comply with California Rule 1.6 confidentiality requirements. A full security overview is available on request.

### Language to avoid
- Don't say "disrupting" court reporting. Firms don't want disruption, they want things to work.
- Don't lead with technology. Lead with time savings and pricing clarity.
- Don't imply reporters are unreliable. Firms have relationships with reporters they value.

## Court reporters (supply side)

### Core message
More work, on your terms. Steady jobs, fast payment, no invoicing.

### Supporting points
- You get paid within 48 hours of delivering the transcript. No invoicing, no collections, no 60-day waits.
- You choose which jobs to accept. Set your own availability, geographic coverage, and turnaround preferences.
- Statutory rates are enforced. The platform cannot and does not discount your per-word rate. Your expedite fees are yours to set.
- W-9 collected once at signup. You never deal with it again for any firm that orders through the platform.
- You are an independent contractor. AB 3136 exempts licensed CSRs from the ABC test. You use your own equipment, set your own schedule, and work through other channels simultaneously.

### Objections to anticipate
- "What's the platform fee?" — The platform charges a fee to the ordering firm, not to you. You receive the full statutory rate for your work.
- "Will this undercut my rates?" — No. Per-word rates are set by statute and enforced by the platform. Expedite fees are set by you. There is no bidding or discounting.
- "Am I an employee?" — No. The engagement is structured as IC under both AB 3136 and the Borello test. You accept or decline work freely, use your own tools, and control your schedule.
- "What if I already work with agencies?" — You can continue working through agencies and through the platform simultaneously. There's no exclusivity.

### Language to avoid
- Don't call it a "gig platform." Reporters take their profession seriously and don't want to be compared to rideshare drivers.
- Don't describe reporters as "vendors" or "providers." They're licensed professionals.
- Don't lead with the technology. Lead with payment speed and job flow.

## Court Reporters Board (CRB)

### Core message
We're building a platform that increases transcript access while reinforcing the professional standards the Board protects.

### Supporting points
- Every reporter on the platform holds an active CSR license, verified against the Board's public database.
- Statutory rates are enforced programmatically. The platform prevents rate violations, it doesn't enable them.
- The platform fee is separate from the reporter's compensation. Reporters receive the full statutory rate.
- IC classification follows AB 3136 and Borello. The platform does not create employer-employee relationships.
- We welcome the Board's input on verification procedures and any concerns about how the platform interacts with licensing rules.

### Objections to anticipate
- "Does this commoditize court reporting?" — No. Reporters are matched based on qualifications, availability, and geographic coverage. The platform doesn't treat them as interchangeable units.
- "What about quality control?" — The platform does not edit or review transcripts. The certified transcript is the reporter's professional work product. Over time, reliability ratings (on-time delivery, acceptance rates) create accountability without interfering with professional judgment.

### Language to avoid
- Don't frame the Board as an obstacle. They're a potential ally whose mission aligns with yours.
- Don't use marketplace jargon (ratings, reviews, competition) when talking to regulators. Use "accountability," "transparency," "compliance."

## Judicial Council / Administrative Office of the Courts

### Core message
We can help close the transcript gap without requiring legislative action, additional court funding, or changes to the ER program.

### Supporting points
- Over 1 million proceedings without a verbatim record in ten months. The platform connects existing reporters with firms that need transcripts, increasing utilization of the licensed workforce.
- Semi-retired CSRs — roughly half the current workforce — can take on remote transcription work through the platform without returning to courtroom attendance.
- The platform supports TRF-eligible orders by simplifying the ordering workflow and auto-generating the reimbursement application paperwork.
- We can provide aggregate data on transcript ordering patterns, turnaround times, and geographic demand that may be useful for the Council's policy work.
- The platform complements the ER program. Electronic recording solves the in-courtroom problem; the platform solves the downstream transcription problem.

### Objections to anticipate
- "Does this reduce the incentive to hire court-employed reporters?" — No. The platform exists because of the shortage, not as an alternative to solving it. Staff-reporter counties still need their own reporters. The platform helps those reporters get paid faster and firms order more easily.
- "What about counties that don't want private reporters?" — In staff-reporter counties, the platform simply streamlines the ordering process for the court's own reporters. It doesn't introduce outside reporters where the court doesn't want them.

### Language to avoid
- Don't criticize the court system's handling of the reporter shortage. The Council is aware of the problem and dealing with political pressure from multiple directions.
- Don't frame the platform as a fix for systemic issues. Frame it as a tool that helps within the current system.

## California Court Reporters Association (CCRA)

### Core message
We're sending more work to reporters, not undercutting them. Rates are set by statute. We can't discount.

### Supporting points
- The platform creates new demand channels for freelance and semi-retired reporters.
- Statutory rates are enforced. There is no bidding, discounting, or race to the bottom.
- Payment within 48 hours of delivery eliminates the collections problem that frustrates many CCRA members.
- The platform can serve as a pathway for recently retired CSRs to continue working on their own terms.
- We're interested in partnering with CCRA for outreach — newsletter mentions, conference presence, or co-branded communications to members.

### Objections to anticipate
- "Marketplaces always drive down prices." — Not when prices are set by statute. The per-word rate is locked by Gov. Code section 69950. The only pricing flexibility is on expedite fees, and those are set by the reporter.
- "You're replacing agencies." — We're an alternative channel. Reporters can use agencies, the platform, and their own direct relationships simultaneously. No exclusivity.
- "Who runs this company? Do they understand court reporting?" — One co-founder is a 22-year California litigator who has lived the transcript ordering process from the firm side. The platform is built by someone who understands the work.

### Language to avoid
- Don't position against agencies. Many CCRA members either are agency reporters or run agencies. Frame as additive, not competitive.
- Don't use Silicon Valley language. "Disruption," "scaling," "leveraging" — none of it. Use "more work," "faster payment," "less admin."

## Legal aid organizations

### Core message
The platform makes transcript access easier for the litigants who need it most.

### Supporting points
- TRF-eligible orders are supported as a workflow feature. The firm or SRL pays the reporter through the platform at the statutory rate, and the platform generates the TRF reimbursement application with all required fields pre-filled.
- Self-represented litigants and fee-waiver clients face the same transcript gap as everyone else, but with fewer resources to navigate it. The platform simplifies the ordering process and the reimbursement paperwork.
- We can provide data on TRF utilization patterns and transcript access rates that legal aid organizations may find useful for advocacy.

### Objections to anticipate
- "Will reporters accept TRF orders through the platform?" — TRF orders work like any other order on the platform. The firm or SRL pays the reporter through the platform upfront, so the reporter gets paid within 48 hours of delivery regardless of TRF reimbursement timing. There is no difference from the reporter's perspective.
- "What's the cost to the litigant?" — The firm or SRL pays the statutory rate plus the platform fee upfront, then files for TRF reimbursement. The TRF reimburses the statutory cost up to $2,500. The platform generates the application paperwork to make filing as simple as possible.
- "Why doesn't the platform front the payment?" — The TRF takes 8-10 weeks to reimburse and has only ~$300K in annual funding. As a pre-revenue startup, we can't responsibly carry that float. We revisit this once we have reserves or outside capital.

### Language to avoid
- Don't assume legal aid orgs will be easy allies. They're protective of their clients and skeptical of for-profit tech platforms. Lead with the access-to-justice angle, not the business model.
- Don't minimize the TRF's limitations ($2,500 cap, 8-10 week processing). Acknowledge them and explain how the platform works within those constraints.
- Don't promise the platform will eventually front TRF payments unless you're confident it will. Frame it as "a future milestone tied to available capital."

## Court administrators (county level)

### Core message
The platform reduces the administrative burden of transcript requests without requiring changes to your court's operations.

### Supporting points
- In staff-reporter counties, the platform streamlines the post-hearing ordering process between firms and your existing reporters. No change to how hearings are staffed.
- In ER counties, the platform provides a structured way for parties to find reporters for audio transcription — a gap that currently has no systematic solution.
- Reduced phone calls and emails to the clerk's office from firms asking how to order transcripts or find reporters.
- Potential for aggregate data on transcript demand in your county that could inform staffing and ER decisions.

### Objections to anticipate
- "We don't want to endorse a private company." — We're not asking for an endorsement. We're asking you to be aware that the platform exists so you can answer questions from firms and reporters who ask about it.
- "Our reporters won't want this." — The platform is opt-in for reporters. We don't impose anything on court-employed reporters who are satisfied with their current workflow.

### Language to avoid
- Don't suggest the court is failing. County administrators are doing their best with limited resources and staffing shortages.
- Don't propose changes to court procedures. The platform works around existing workflows.

## General messaging principles (all audiences)

1. Lead with the problem, not the product. Everyone in the California court system knows the transcript situation is broken. Start there.
2. Statutory rates are your best friend. In every conversation, make clear that per-word rates are set by law and the platform enforces them. This defuses the biggest fear (price undercutting) before it becomes an objection.
3. Rosie is the credibility. A 22-year California litigator built this because she lived the problem. That story does more work than any feature list.
4. The platform is a tool, not a replacement. It doesn't replace reporters, agencies, courts, or existing relationships. It makes the existing system work better.
5. Two-track framing works for everyone. "We help in staff-reporter counties and ER counties, just differently" — this shows sophistication and earns credibility.
