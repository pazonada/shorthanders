## Why carriers matter

Per Rosie (4/11): ~70% of attorneys functionally work for insurance companies. Carriers pay for the defense, audit every line item, and pressure firms to keep costs down. Firms have to submit estimated budgets and negotiate costs of defending each suit. Carriers have entire systems to audit and benchmark what firms typically spend.

**One carrier relationship = dozens or hundreds of panel firms at once.**

This is the highest-leverage sales channel available, but it requires:
1. Proof that the platform saves money (launch-window data)
2. Enough supply to handle volume (250+ CSRs)
3. Someone to do the outreach grind (Aud, Rosie, or a PT hire)

## The pitch to carriers

**Core message:** "Your panel firms are overpaying for court reporting through agencies. Shorthanders connects them directly with licensed reporters at statutory rates – transparent pricing, faster delivery, and cost data you can actually audit."

**What carriers care about:**
- Reducing ALAE (allocated loss adjustment expenses) – court reporting is a line item they audit
- Transparent, benchmarkable costs – statutory rates are public and verifiable
- Less billable attorney time wasted on transcript logistics
- Data: cost per matter, cost by case type, turnaround metrics
- Compliance: all reporters CSR-verified, license checked quarterly

**What we'd offer carriers:**
- Anonymized cost-savings data from our launch cohort (e.g., "firms using Shorthanders saved X% on transcript costs vs. agency rates")
- Carrier-specific reporting dashboard (stretch goal): spend by panel firm, by case type, vs. industry benchmarks
- A direct line to the founders to surface carrier-specific reporting or workflow needs that aren't in V1
- No integration required – panel firms just use the platform at the standard 12% flat fee

## Channels

### 1. Defense firm introductions (warmest path)
- Insurance defense firms already using the platform introduce us to their carrier contacts
- "Our carrier might want to know about this – it's saving us money on transcripts"
- This is bottom-up: the firm evangelizes to the carrier

### 2. Rosie's network
- Does Rosie know anyone on the carrier/TPA side from her 22 years of practice?
- Family law intersects with insurance less often, but she may have contacts from PI or employment cases

### 3. LinkedIn outreach to carrier litigation management
- Target: "Litigation Manager," "Vendor Manager," "Director of Claims" at CA carriers
- Share content about transcript cost transparency, ALAE reduction
- Aud's marketing/comms background is perfect for this

### 4. Industry events (when budget allows)
- CLM (Claims and Litigation Management Alliance) – annual conference + regional events
- PLUS (Professional Liability Underwriting Society)
- RIMS (Risk Management Society)
- Booth space is $2K-$10K+ – not feasible now, but attending as a participant is often free or low-cost
- Goal: meet 3-5 carrier contacts per event

### 5. PT hire for outreach grind (when funded)
- Junior BD associate with insurance or legal vendor background
- $2,000-$3,000/month, 15 hrs/week
- Research carrier contacts, cold outreach, set up calls for Rosie/Aud
- Don't hire until there's revenue to support it

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Long sales cycles (6-12 months) | Start planting seeds early, don't depend on carrier revenue for survival |
| Carriers want integrations we can't build yet | Start with "no integration required" – just a vendor recommendation |
| Carriers want exclusivity or preferred rates | Negotiate carefully – don't give away margin for volume we can't yet handle |
| Small platform can't handle carrier-driven volume spike | Only pursue carrier deals when supply (250+ CSRs) can support it |
