## Two-stage research approach

Per Aud's 2026-05-13 plan: CSR research happens in two stages.

| Stage | Format | Audience size | Time per respondent | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 – Survey (this doc)** | Anonymous web form (Google Forms / Typeform) | 30–60 responses | 5–7 minutes | Broad feedback on concept, pricing, features. Quantitative + short-answer. |
| **2 – Prototype testing** | 1:1 video call walkthrough – see [user_testing_csr_guide.md](user_testing_csr_guide.md) | 5 selected from survey respondents | 60–75 minutes | Deep qualitative reactions to the actual product flow. |

The survey opt-in invites respondents to volunteer for follow-up prototype testing. Stage 2 participants are selected from that pool to match a mix of work types, experience levels, and geographies.

**Important split (added 2026-05-13):** the conceptual / business-model / pricing questions that were originally part of [user_testing_csr_guide.md](user_testing_csr_guide.md) have been **absorbed into this survey** so that Stage 2 prototype-testing sessions can stay focused on site content and UX – not abstract concept feedback. Stage 1 establishes the business validation; Stage 2 validates the actual product.

---

## Survey design notes

- **Keep it under 7 minutes.** CSRs are busy professionals; longer surveys collapse the response rate.
- **Mostly multiple choice and short numeric.** One or two free-text fields for richer feedback.
- **Anonymous by default**, optional contact field for follow-up.
- **No pricing-anchoring at the top.** Lead with their current reality, then introduce DS concepts. Avoids biasing answers.
- **Built in Google Forms or Typeform.** Both support conditional logic if needed.
- **Distribution:** Rosie's network, CSR Facebook groups (Court Reporter Rates Discussion + CA-specific groups), Cal-CCRA outreach. Target 30–60 responses over 2 weeks.

### Section 4 – What you think of Dewey Sholes (6 questions)

**Read this first, then answer the questions below.**

> Dewey Sholes is a new website that lets California attorneys hire court reporters directly for live court hearings. (We can also handle audio transcription in the rare cases where no reporter was on the record and the court approves releasing the audio.)
>
> Here's how it works for you, the reporter:
> - You keep 100% of the platform's published rate. No agency cut. No platform commission on your earnings.
> - At launch, every reporter on the platform earns the same published rate. We've set those rates well above the statutory minimums and close to what private-direct work pays. (Specific dollar amounts in Section 5.)
> - Attorneys pay your rate plus a flat 12% platform fee on top, so the 12% never comes out of your pocket.
> - The site handles scheduling, payment, and transcript delivery, so you don't pay an agency to do those things for you.
>
> *Looking ahead: we're planning to add a feature later where you'd be able to set your own rate within a range around the platform default. We want to launch with simple flat pricing first, then build that out once we know what reporters and attorneys actually want. Section 5 asks what you'd think of that.*

## What we're trying to learn from each section

| Section | What success looks like | What failure looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 – Background | Mix of years, work types, counties; meaningful WC representation (Q2a) | All respondents look the same – survey isn't reaching the whole CSR community |
| 2 – Workflow & pain (new) | Lost-transcript-order is real (Q5c >50% say yes); post-hearing handoff is messy (Q5a) – validates the business-card / direct-relationship thesis | Workflow is already smooth; we're solving a non-problem |
| 3 – Current economics | Real data on agency splits + direct rates – fills the [pricing_questions_rosie_and_csrs.md](pricing_questions_rosie_and_csrs.md) Q6/Q7 gap that Rosie couldn't answer | Most respondents say "I don't know" – we still need user testing to learn |
| 4 – Concept reaction | Top concerns are addressable (volume, trust, payment) and excitement is real (no agency cut, direct relationships) | Top concerns are existential (concept itself rejected, "agencies are fine") |
| 4 – Featured reporter (Q14) | Meaningful interest (>40% say "yes") – validates Rosie's idea | Low interest (<20%) – defer indefinitely |
| 5 – Pricing perception | 12% feels reasonable; 20% rate-setting band feels like meaningful control | Either rejected – needs rethinking before sending firms |
| 6 – Signup + referrals (Q19, Q19a, Q20) | Majority say Yes or Maybe at $99/yr; Q19a free text identifies a handful of high-leverage perks that move Maybe/No to Yes; strong referral willingness | <30% Yes at $99/yr – supply acquisition harder than projected at this price, or perks aren't compelling enough |

## Distribution plan

- **Soft launch:** Rosie sends to 10 trusted CSR contacts first to catch survey-design issues. 1 week to refine.
- **Wave 1:** Cal-CCRA mailing list, Rosie's professional network, CSR Facebook groups (Court Reporter Rates Discussion is 11K members – Caldarella's audience). 2 weeks.
- **Wave 2:** Boost via paid LinkedIn promotion targeting CA CSRs ($100 budget). 1 week.
- **Thank-you for completion:** $10 digital gift card to first 50 completers (caps cost at $500). Or skip if response rate is strong organically.

Target n = 30–60 completed responses. Subset of ~10 volunteers self-select for Stage 2 prototype testing.

## What to revisit after Stage 1 results

The pricing-question design (Q19 + Q19a) tests willingness to pay at the actual $99/yr we're charging. Once we have 30+ responses, we'll know:

- Whether $99/yr is a hard "no" at this audience or a "maybe" that perks can unlock
- Which perks have actual leverage (the free-text follow-up reveals this)
- Whether the founder-access / roadmap-input perks we're offering as founding-member benefits map to anything CSRs actually value

**Decisions deferred until after Stage 1:**
- Do we keep the $99/yr price, lower it, or layer in tiered options based on the demand curve we see?
- Do we add a launch-window incentive (e.g., first-year discount) if the no-rate becomes a real signup barrier?
- Do the founding-cohort perks need to be rebuilt around what CSRs surface in Q19a free text?

We deliberately retired the prior pilot-pricing tier (waived $99/yr first year, then bumps up) before this survey ships, because the discounted-then-bumps-up model creates a churn cliff and the survey results would have been muddied by the implicit "but only for a year" framing. Cleaner to test $99/yr directly and let the survey tell us if we need to revisit.
