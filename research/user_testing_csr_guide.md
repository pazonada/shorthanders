**Why Stage 0 was added (2026-05-24).** The business model went through several iterations in May 2026 – from job-board "first-claim-wins" to cascade-with-veto-window to applicant-pool review. We have no validation signal that the *current* model resonates with working CSRs. Before sending the survey at scale, Aud will have 3–5 unstructured conversations to listen for whether the model lands, what's confusing, and what assumptions we've made that don't survive contact with reality. The survey gets refined based on those conversations.

The Stage 2 prototype walkthrough doesn't re-litigate the model – it tests whether the *actual product* delivers on the model. Participants for Stage 2 are recruited from Stage 1 respondents who opted in.

## Stage 0 – Validate the model (before survey)

**Format:** 30-minute calls (Zoom or phone), unstructured. Recorded with consent. Aud facilitates; Rosie joins when scheduling allows.

**Sample size:** 3–5 working CSRs. Mix of live attendance and audio transcription. At least one who's been doing this 15+ years and at least one newly licensed (within 3 years) – different relationships to platforms.

**Recruitment:** Rosie's network + CSR shortage action group + one or two referrals. Compensation: $50 gift card per call.

### What we're validating

The current business model has four moving parts that haven't been tested with working CSRs in their current form:

1. **Open marketplace pricing.** CSRs set their own rate per line item (per-page, half-day, full-day, trial day, travel/hour). The platform shows a regional baseline as guidance only. No platform-imposed cap.
2. **Applicant-pool review.** CSRs apply to jobs they want; the firm reviews everyone who applied and picks one (or none). The firm sees profiles + rates and picks based on whatever criteria they want.
3. **Live viewing + selection by the firm.** The firm can pick an applicant any time – not gated by the application window closing. Favorites + pre-selection messaging mean some firms will pick a favorite within minutes of posting.
4. **Favorite-from notifications.** When a firm that has favorited a CSR posts a new job, the CSR gets a louder push + email alert.

### Discussion script (unstructured, but cover these)

**Warm up (5 min)**
- How do you currently find court reporting work? Walk me through a typical week.
- What works? What's broken?
- How do you set your rate today? Is it different for different attorneys / case types / counties?

**The model (15 min) – describe in plain language, then listen**

Describe the model one piece at a time, pausing for reactions. Don't sell. The goal is to surface concerns, confusions, and "I would never do that" reactions, not to get agreement.

- *"On the platform, you set your own rates – per page, half day, full day, trial day, travel per hour. We show what most reporters in your region charge as guidance, but you set the actual number. Reactions?"*
  - Listen for: do they understand "set your own rate" as an opportunity or a burden? Do they price differently by case type? Would they want a different number per attorney they work with?
- *"When a law firm needs a reporter, they post the job and we notify reporters whose preferences match. You see the job in your dashboard. If you want it, you click Apply. We don't pick for you – the firm sees everyone who applied and chooses one based on their profile and rate. Reactions?"*
  - Listen for: do they get the "apply, don't claim" framing? Do they feel like the firm has too much power? Do they feel like they have to compete on price? Does it feel like real work to apply, or one-click?
- *"The firm can pick whoever they want, whenever they want – including five minutes after posting if they already know who they want. We tell qualified reporters about the job at the same time, but a firm with a clear favorite can move fast. The window we show (4 hours for rush, 24 for expedited, 72 for standard) is when we ping everyone, not when the firm has to wait. Reactions?"*
  - Listen for: do they feel like favorites get unfair priority? Or is that just how this work has always operated? Does "you might apply and then see the firm pick someone in 5 minutes" feel demoralizing?
- *"If a firm has worked with you before and marked you as a favorite, you get a louder notification when they post – push to your phone plus email, both on by default. You can turn it off in settings if it's too much. Reactions?"*
  - Listen for: how loud is too loud? Are they comfortable being on the favorite list of 5 firms and getting 5 louder pings? Would they want any way to mute a specific firm?

**The whole picture (10 min)**
- *"If I described this to a CSR friend who hasn't heard of Shorthanders, would they be interested? Skeptical? Why?"*
- *"What's the single biggest reason a working CSR would say 'no thanks' to this?"*
- *"If we built this exactly the way I described, would you sign up at launch? What would you want to know before deciding?"*
- *"What would change your mind if you started as a no?"*

### What we're listening for (facilitator notes)

- **Pricing fluency.** Do CSRs already think in per-line-item rates, or do they default to a single hourly / per-diem number? Does asking for 5 separate inputs (per-page, half, full, trial, travel) feel right or excessive?
- **Apply vs. claim framing.** Does "apply to a job" feel natural, or paternalistic ("we have to apply to get our own work")?
- **Competition with peers.** Are CSRs OK seeing other CSRs' rates publicly? Is there a culture of not undercutting? Will the marketplace dynamic make rates race to the bottom?
- **Favorites as visible label.** Are CSRs comfortable being publicly tagged as a firm's favorite? Or is that a label they'd want hidden from other firms / other CSRs?
- **Notification load.** Push + email both on for favorite-from is sticky-on by default. Is that aggressive in a good way ("I won't miss work I want") or a bad way ("my phone is on fire all day")?
- **What's missing.** Anything they expected to hear that we didn't describe? That's a hint about what their mental model is.

### Output of Stage 0

Before the survey goes live, Aud writes a 1-page memo:
- What landed
- What concerned working CSRs
- What we got wrong about how this work currently operates
- What changes (if any) we should make to the model OR the survey before Stage 1

If the model doesn't survive Stage 0, we revisit before sending the survey at scale.

## Stage 2 – Prototype walkthrough

**Where this fits.** Stage 0 validated (or refined) the model. Stage 1 captured concept reaction at scale. Stage 2 tests the *actual product*.

### What we're trying to learn (Stage 2 specifically)

Six concrete site / UX questions that require seeing the actual product:

1. **Does the public CSR profile feel right** to a working CSR? Cases-worked display, job-completion count, rating breakdown – comfortable or invasive?
2. **Does the reporter onboarding flow have friction** points that would cause real CSRs to bail?
3. **Does the apply flow** (Available jobs → Apply → Your applications) match what CSRs need to make a fast in-the-moment decision?
4. **Does the favorite-from notification** feel right? Visible enough? Too aggressive? Easy to manage?
5. **Does the business-cards concept work in practice** – would CSRs actually hand them out? What would they want them to say?
6. **What's missing from the platform** that a working CSR would expect on day one but we haven't built?

### Method

**Format:** 45-minute video calls (Zoom or Google Meet). Recorded with consent.
**Sample size:** Up to 5 completed interviews. Launch-stage scale; not aiming for statistical significance, just signal.
**Compensation:** $25 Starbucks gift card per completed interview. Sent digitally within 24 hours.
**Tools:** Zoom recording + transcript, simple notes doc, lightweight participant tracker.

**Recruitment criteria (mix within 5 slots – prioritize live attendance since that's the cards-strategy target):**
- 2-3 CSRs who do mostly **live attendance** in CA superior courts
- 1-2 CSRs who do **audio transcription** primarily
- At least 1 **newly licensed** CSR (within last 24 months) if possible – different incentive structure, no entrenched book of business
- Solo CSRs preferred over those embedded in agencies; we want decision-makers

**Recruitment channel (primary):**
- Stage 1 survey respondents who opted into Q21 follow-up. Filter by work-type mix above.

**Backup channels** (if Stage 1 doesn't produce enough opt-ins):
- Rosie's professional network
- CSR shortage action group contacts
- Direct outreach to recently licensed CSRs from the CRB directory

### Discussion guide

Total target: ~45 minutes. Time budgets are guidance, not hard caps.

**Warm-up (2-3 min)**

- Quick recap from your survey response: you do mostly [X], in [Y counties], [Z years experience]. Anything that's changed since you filled out the survey, or anything you wanted to expand on?
- Anything you've been thinking about since the survey that you want to flag before we walk through the site?

**Landing page + onboarding flow (8-10 min)**

Walk through on screen-share. Do not over-explain – let them react.

- Show landing page. *Reactions: clear or confusing, anything missing, would you scroll further?*
- Show the reporter onboarding flow up to where the prototype gets unrealistic.
  - *Where in the flow do you start to second-guess?*
  - *Anything you'd expect to see that isn't there?*
  - *Any friction points where a real CSR would bail?*

**CSR profile page (10-12 min)**

This is the highest-leverage UX surface. Take the time.

- What do you think of having a public profile like this?
- Are you comfortable with attorneys seeing your name, license number, and rating?
- **Open question A:** Would you want recent cases you've worked on shown publicly here? Why or why not?
- **Open question B:** Would you want a count of jobs you've completed on the platform shown? Does that help you or hurt you?
- **Open question C:** How do you feel about the "Additional expertise" free-text field vs. the standardized specialty checkboxes? Which would you actually fill out?
- What's missing from this profile that would make you more comfortable / more useful?

**Invoice builder + getting paid (12-15 min) – HIGH PRIORITY, go slow**

The core "get paid" surface in the reduced-scope prototype. Walk it with the reporter's own
real billing in mind: the Payments tab, the Invoices tab, starting a new invoice (template pick →
builder → line items → surcharges → preview → send), and the shared invoice modal / paid receipt.
Do **not** over-explain – have them narrate what they'd do and where it doesn't match their world.
Several of these are **live design questions** we're deciding right now, so their instinct matters.

- First: walk me through how you invoice today. What tool? What does a typical invoice look like –
  which line items, in what order, and what does the firm expect to see?
- **Open question K – the fields.** Do the invoice fields match how you actually bill? What's
  missing, what's extra? (Probe: matter/case reference, service vs. delivery date, terms/due date,
  tax, your license/cert on the face of the invoice, remit-to, PO number.)
- **Open question L – the math (metered vs. flat).** For each line, does "quantity × rate" fit, or
  is it a single flat fee? Which of your items are truly per-page (need a page count) vs. one-off
  fees (appearance/per-diem, minimum, certification, expedited)? Should we **hide the quantity on
  flat-fee lines** so a minimum fee can't get multiplied? Are any of yours per-hour (realtime,
  waiting) or per-mile (travel) – i.e. metered but not per-page?
- **Open question M – rate prefill.** We can pre-fill each line's rate from the rates on your
  profile. Helpful, or would you rather type it fresh? Do your rates vary enough by job / attorney /
  county that a saved default would be wrong more often than right?
- **Open question N – surcharges & templates.** Are the surcharge add-ons (realtime, expedited,
  rough draft, travel, waiting time) the right set? What else do you add? Would you use saved
  templates, and do the per-regime starters (deposition / workers' comp / court-audio) match how you
  think about jobs?
- **Open question O – expedited / rush.** How do you actually bill rush – a flat fee, a per-page
  bump, or a percentage of the transcript? (We keep it manual today; this decides whether we build a
  percentage calculator.)
- **Open question P – the receipt / paid state.** Show a paid invoice (the modal + green PAID stamp +
  Print). Is this a good enough receipt for you and for the firm's records? Do you need to print /
  PDF / email it? Anything an attorney's accounts-payable would demand that isn't here?
- **Open question Q – routing payment through us.** Your fee comes out of the payment (2% / 5%); the
  firm sees only what you billed. Does that model make sense? Would you route real payments through
  here instead of your current method – and what would stop you?
- **Open question R – payment terms & what "overdue" means.** Do you put payment terms on your
  invoices – net-30 / net-60 / net-90, due on receipt? Does it vary by firm? Firms often pay on their
  own accounts-payable cycle regardless. So when should an invoice show as **overdue** – a set number
  of days after you send it, a due date you pick per invoice, or is "overdue" not something we should
  flag at all (since you can't control when a firm pays)? Would a **terms field** (net-N that fills in
  the due date for you) be useful, or just noise? *(This is a live design decision – their answer
  shapes whether we add a terms field and how the "Awaiting payment" / overdue logic works.)*
- What's missing from the invoicing flow that you'd need on day one?

**Available jobs + Apply flow (10-12 min)**

Show the CSR dashboard – Available jobs tab. Frame: "When firms post orders, qualified reporters see them here. Click Apply on the ones you want; the firm reviews everyone who applied and picks one."

- *Reactions: is the information on the job card enough to decide whether to apply?*
- **Open question D:** What information about the firm / case would help you decide whether to apply? (Probe: budget signal, urgency signal, firm reputation, anything they're not seeing.)
- **Open question E:** Once you apply, what would you want to see in the "Your applications" tab? Right now we show status (waiting / firm reviewing / selected / job filled). Useful? Missing anything?
- **Open question F:** Imagine you applied at 9am and the firm picked someone else at 9:05am – before any of the other applicants probably even saw the job. How does that feel? Demoralizing? Fine? Walk me through it.
- **Open question G:** The application window is 4h for rush jobs, 24h for expedited, 72h for standard. Do those feel right? Too long? Too short? Especially: would you want longer for standard jobs so you have time to read details, or do you decide fast?

**Favorite-from notifications + "Favorited by X firms" panel (5-7 min)**

Show the Favorited-by-firms banner on the CSR dashboard ("You're a favorite at 4 firms"). Frame: "When a firm has favorited you and posts a job, you get a louder push + email alert – both defaulted on. You can turn either off in settings."

- *Reactions: does seeing 'You're a favorite at 4 firms' feel good? Strange? Inaccurate to how favoriting works in your world?*
- **Open question H:** Are push + email both defaulted on too aggressive, or about right? Would you want them off until you opted in instead?
- **Open question I:** If you're favorited by 10 firms and they post a lot, you could be getting many louder pings per day. Is that a problem? Would you want to mute a specific firm's notifications without unfavoriting?

**Business cards concept (5-7 min)**

Show mockup: card with the CSR's name, license number, profile QR code, "Find me on Shorthanders" tagline.

- **Open question J:** Would you actually hand these out at the end of a hearing? In what context?
- Would the platform branding bother you? Would you prefer a card with no platform branding (just your info + a generic site URL)?
- What would you want the card to say?
- *(If they don't already hand out business cards – survey Q5d) Would this change that?*

**Reaching firms directly (roadmap probe, 3-4 min)**

- **Open question S:** Today firms find and message reporters. Would you want it the other way too –
  **browse profiles of law firms that have signed up, and message them directly to pitch your
  services**? Walk me through when you'd actually use that. *(Roadmap item – "reverse discovery."
  Listen for: is proactively pitching firms something CSRs do or want, or does it feel like
  cold-selling that's beneath them / against etiquette? Which firms would they reach out to – ones
  they know, or new ones? Does having it change how valuable the platform is to them? Validate
  before building, same as the firm-side personalization direction.)*

**Anything missing (3-5 min)**

- Walk me through what you'd expect to be able to do on this site on a typical work day. Does what we've shown you cover it?
- What's missing from the site that you'd expect to find on day one?
- Anything you want to flag that I didn't ask about?

**Wrap (2-3 min)**

- Can we follow up if we have more questions?
- Would you be willing to test a later iteration of the site in 2–3 months?

## Open questions we need answered (track per-interview)

These are the specific things prototype testing is explicitly designed to answer. Concept-level questions (will CSRs use this, what kills trust, pricing perception) are tracked in Stage 0 + Stage 1 results.

| Question | Hypothesis to test | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|
| Are CSRs comfortable with public profiles showing recent cases? | Mixed – some will want it, some won't. | Whether to show recent cases on profile in v1.1 or make it opt-in only. |
| Are CSRs comfortable with public profiles showing job-count? | Likely uncomfortable for new CSRs (cold-start penalty). | Whether to show job-count on profile, ever. |
| Does the "Additional expertise" free-text get used in practice? | CSRs who have niche expertise will fill it; standard specialists will leave it blank. | Whether to keep the field or hide it. |
| Will CSRs actually distribute business cards post-hearing? | Yes, especially for CSRs trying to build their own book. | Whether to invest in card production at all. |
| Do the per-tier application windows feel realistic? | 4h Rush is tight but workable; 24h Expedited and 72h Standard are fine. | Whether to adjust per-tier windows pre-launch. |
| Is the "firm picks within minutes" scenario demoralizing or fine? | Mixed – some will feel it's how this work has always operated, others will feel cheated. | Whether to add a delay before firms can select, or to surface "X new applicants applied after you" as transparency. |
| Are the favorite-from defaults too aggressive? | Probably fine for CSRs favorited by 1–3 firms; possibly too loud at 5+. | Whether to add a per-firm mute affordance in v1.1. |
| Where does the onboarding flow lose people? | First friction point: license-verification step (assumed easy but may have edge cases). | Where to invest in onboarding polish. |
